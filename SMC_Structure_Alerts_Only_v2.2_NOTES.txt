v2.2 — PULLBACK RULE 3: two candles once the pullback takes out the
opposite level                                     (built on v2.1)

WHAT YOU REPORTED
The second iBOS was drawn at a lower high than the obvious spike high, and the
iCHOCH at a shallower low than the obvious swing low.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY HAPPENING - one cause, not two.

  THE iBOS. The internal ceiling is filled here:

      if not na(aPhP) and aPhB > iAncB and close < aPhP and (...)
          if na(iCeilP)            <-- only ever fills an EMPTY slot
              iCeilP := aPhP

  aPhP is a CONFIRMED pivot high. The spike high never became one, because the
  pullback after it produced two counting candles and the rule wanted three. The
  slot therefore stayed empty until the next high that did manage three - the
  lower one - and that took it. And because of "if na(iCeilP)", a higher pivot
  arriving later CANNOT replace a ceiling that is already set. Only a
  sweep-driven pivot-2 promotion can.

  THE iCHOCH. Not chosen as a pivot at all. When the iBOS fires, the floor is
  re-anchored to f_retExtInc(iCeilB, false) - the lowest low FROM THE CEILING
  PIVOT'S BAR ONWARD. Your deep wick sits before that bar, so it was outside the
  search window by construction. It is downstream of the first problem: fix the
  ceiling and this corrects itself.

  Your existing "two candles is enough on a sweep" behaviour did not apply here.
  It lives in rearm() and armHere(), which seed the counter to 1 when the tracker
  is RE-ARMED - the opposite pivot has just confirmed, or price swept back
  through the last confirmed swing. It has never applied mid-pullback, so a big
  candle sweeping a low while the count was already running got no credit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE NEW RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Settings: "Market structure - mode & inducement (IDM)", below pullback rules 1
and 2. OFF by default.

    PULLBACK RULE 3 - two candles are enough once the pullback takes out the
    opposite live level (CHOCH* / iCHOCH*)

Hunting a HIGH, the swing confirms on the SECOND counted pullback candle instead
of the third, provided some bar since that high was set has traded its LOW below
the live level underneath - CHOCH* for main structure, iCHOCH* for internal and
inner. Mirrored hunting a LOW against BOS* / iBOS*. A wick through is enough; it
does not have to close through.

The sweep is only watched AFTER the high or low being hunted is set. A new
extreme restarts the hunt and clears the flag, so the sweep always has to happen
on the way back from the high you are trying to confirm, never on the bar that
made it.

SCOPE - all three tiers, each against its own pair of live levels, plus the
multi-timeframe engine against its own:

    main trackers          -> florS.price / ceilS.price   (CHOCH* / BOS*)
    alternating trackers   -> iFlorP / iCeilP             (iCHOCH* / iBOS*)
    f_tfPack main          -> tFlor / tCeil
    f_tfPack alternating   -> iFl / iCe

The alternating pair feeds BOTH the internal and the inner tier - there is one
pivot stream, not two - so inner pivots are judged against the internal levels
as well. That is how the engine has always been wired; this rule does not change
it.

HOW IT IS SWITCHED OFF. The level is handed to the counter at the CALL SITE and
is na when the toggle is off:

    [_ph, _phb] = aHiT.update(pbSwp2 ? iFlorP : na)

With na there, the swept flag can never be set and the new branch can never be
reached. That is why the defaults are provably untouched rather than merely
probably untouched.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEASURED EFFECT - READ THIS BEFORE TICKING IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Over 300 random-walk runs of 400 bars with an alternating tracker pair:

    rule 3 off                          baseline
    rule 3 on                           +83% pivots confirmed
    rule 3 AND rule 1 on together       +3% only

Two things to take from that.

FIRST, +83% is a lot. It is also an UPPER BOUND: the simulation feeds the last
confirmed opposite pivot as the sweep level, which moves more often than the real
iFlorP does, so the real figure will be lower. Even so, expect materially more
iBOS/iCHOCH and nBOS/nCHOCH marks, and more alerts with them. Look at a chart
before you put it on an alert.

SECOND, and this is the important one: RULE 3 AND RULE 1 LARGELY CANCEL. Rule 1
throws out any candle whose body is smaller than its longest wick - and a candle
big enough to sweep the opposite level usually has a long wick, which is exactly
the candle rule 3 exists to reward. Run rule 3 ON ITS OWN first. If you want
both, check on a chart that the sweeps you care about still count.

================================================================================
v2.1 — FIX: "The requested historical offset is beyond the historical
buffer's limit"                                    (built on v2.0)
================================================================================

WHAT YOU SAW
    Error on bar 14031: The requested historical offset (1095) is beyond the
    historical buffer's limit (1094).
        at f_hLine()
        at #main()

WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS - and it was my bug, in every version since the live 50%
line was added.

TradingView keeps only a limited window of past bars addressable by a script.
When you hand a drawing a bar index - line.set_xy1(line, x, y) - Pine turns that
index into an offset from the current bar and reads it out of that window. Ask
for a bar older than the window and the whole indicator stops with this error.

f_hLine draws the six live levels: BOS* / CHOCH*, iBOS* / iCHOCH*,
nBOS* / nCHOCH* and the three 50% lines. It pinned each line's left end to the
pivot the level came from, and it bounded that with "Max history to draw"
(histBars), which defaults to 5000 and can be set to 20000.

histBars is not the buffer. It is a display window, and it was never the right
thing to bound a drawing coordinate with. The buffer on your chart was 1094
bars. So the moment a live level stayed put for more than about 1094 bars - a
range that holds, which is completely normal - the line's left end pointed
outside the window and the script died.

WHY THE 10-MARKS CAP DID NOT HELP
You had "keep only the latest 10 marks per tier" ticked and 15-20 drawings on
the chart, and still got it. That is expected and it is the clue that pins the
diagnosis: the cap only ever trims HISTORY. The six live lines are the current
levels - they are deliberately never counted or deleted by it. The crash came
from one of those six, not from the marks the cap governs. Nothing you could
have set in the panel would have avoided it.

WHY IT LOOKED RANDOM
It depends on how old the current structure range is, not on how much is drawn.
Quiet, range-bound stretches age the anchor; a fresh break resets it. So it
fires on some symbols and timeframes and not others, and it can clear itself
when the next break lands.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two new helpers, declared before anything that draws:

    int _xBack = 1500
    f_xClamp(int b) => na(b) ? bar_index : math.max(math.min(b, bar_index), bar_index - _xBack)
    f_xNear(int b)  => not na(b) and b <= bar_index and (bar_index - b) <= _xBack

and max_bars_back raised from 500 to 2000, so a 1500-bar reach is always inside
the buffer with 500 bars to spare.

They are applied by kind, because the right answer differs:

  CLAMP - for lines that extend to the right, where only the left end moves and
  the level itself is unchanged. f_hLine, mkSLine (the live BOS*/CHOCH* line),
  freeze() and repos(). A level older than 1500 bars now starts 1500 bars back
  instead of at its pivot. A chart shows 200-400 bars, so you will not see it.

  SKIP - for marks that sit ON a specific old bar, where clamping would put the
  mark at a bar it does not belong to. f_intMark, f_innMark, f_intStrong,
  f_innStrong, f_idmMark, the pivot dots, and the two strong-point stamps. These
  are all created at the moment of the break, when the pivot is recent, so the
  skip is very nearly unreachable - and a single missing mark beats the whole
  indicator stopping.

The engine is untouched. Every break, every trend, every alert is computed
exactly as before; this only governs where a drawing may be anchored.

PROVED, NOT ASSUMED
f_hLine's clamp was run over its whole input space - bar_index from 0 to 15000,
last_bar_index at four offsets, histBars at 300 / 500 / 5000 / 20000, and the
anchor at every interesting position including na and beyond the current bar.
Worst offset produced: exactly 1500, against a 2000 buffer. Same for f_xClamp.
It cannot ask for a bar outside the window under any settings.

Also checked: nine functions changed, two added, nothing else differs from
v2.0; no new unresolved identifiers; indentation, brackets, empty-block and
duplicate-declaration scans clean; and a grep for series history read at a
computed offset (close[n], time[n] and friends) found none anywhere in the
file - drawing coordinates were the only exposure in the structure engine.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
v2.1 ALSO FIXES A COMPILE ERROR THAT HAD BEEN IN THIS FILE SINCE v1.0
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Error at 43:23  Input's "defval" value must be between "minval" and "maxval"

When this alert-only build was first cut, "Max history to draw" had its default
lowered from 5000 to 300 to keep the drawing budget small - but the floor that
came with it from the full indicator, minval = 500, was left alone. 300 is below
500, so Pine refused the input and the script never compiled. v1.0 and v2.0 both
carried it; neither of them could ever have run.

The floor is now minval = 100. The default stays at 300, and you can take it
lower still if you want the leanest possible alert copy.

This is the kind of fault a compile catches in one second and a reader does not,
so it is now covered by a checker that runs over every file before I hand it
over: for every input it re-does Pine's own build-time validation - defval inside
minval/maxval, minval below maxval, step above zero, and a string defval that is
actually one of its options. Run across all nine scripts in this project it found
exactly one fault, this one, in the three alert-only builds and nowhere else.

ONE PLACE I HAVE NOT TOUCHED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The volume-profile and killzone renderers anchor their boxes and POC/VA lines to

on a 1-minute or 5-minute chart, where a day is 1440 or 288 bars. Say the word

================================================================================
WHAT CHANGED IN v2.0   (built on v1.0)
================================================================================

Three changes, and nothing else. Every function in the file except the pullback
counter is byte-for-byte identical to v1.0, and the two new switches are OFF by
default, so a fresh copy behaves exactly like v1.0 did.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PULLBACK RULE 1 — body bigger than its own longest wick
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Settings: "Market structure - mode & inducement (IDM)" group, below the
ALTERNATE switch.

The normal three-candle pullback is unchanged. This adds one extra demand on top
of it: each of the three candles must satisfy

    body      = |close - open|
    longest wick = the larger of
                     high - max(open, close)      (upper wick)
                     min(open, close) - low       (lower wick)
    the candle counts only while   body > longest wick

A candle that fails does NOT reset the count. It simply does not count, exactly
the way a wrong-direction candle behaves today, and the sequence keeps waiting.
That was a deliberate choice: resetting would have been far stricter than what
you asked for.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PULLBACK RULE 2 — close beyond the previous candle's BODY, not its wick
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same group, directly below rule 1.

The normal rule asks each pullback candle to close beyond the PREVIOUS pullback
candle's wick: below its low when hunting a high, above its high when hunting a
low.

Rule 2 moves that reference onto the body: below the previous candle's body
bottom when hunting a high, above its body top when hunting a low. Because a
pullback candle is by definition bearish (hunting a high) or bullish (hunting a
low), its body edge IS its close - so in practice it becomes "each candle closes
beyond the last one's close".

Rule 2 also carries rule 1's body-bigger-than-its-wick demand, whether or not
rule 1 is ticked. So it is LOOSER on where the close lands and STRICTER on the
quality of the candle.

WITH BOTH TICKED, rule 2 decides the reference level. The body demand is
identical in both, so they never contradict each other.

MEASURED EFFECT (120,000 simulated bars per direction, random walk):
    default                 baseline
    rule 1 only             41% fewer swings confirmed
    rule 2 only             27% fewer
    both ticked             27% fewer - identical to rule 2 alone, which
                            confirms rule 2 supersedes rule 1 as described

Both rules apply to MAIN, INTERNAL and INNER at once, and to the multi-timeframe
engine, because all four run on the same pullback counter. There is no way for
the tiers to disagree with each other.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. FIXED: the ALTERNATE rule now reaches INTERNAL and INNER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BUG. "ALTERNATE rule: BOS is valid only AFTER IDM" only ever gated the MAIN
tier. The internal and inner engines were computing their inducement levels and
their idm-hit flags on every break - the machinery was all there and running -
but nothing ever consulted it. So turning ALTERNATE on changed the main
BOS/CHOCH marks and left iBOS/iCHOCH and nBOS/nCHOCH exactly as they were.

THE FIX. Both tiers now do what the main tier has always done, in both halves of
the rule:

  a) The break gate. An internal or inner BOS is now only valid once its
     inducement has been taken:

        if close > iCeilP and (iTrend != 1 or not altMode or iCeilIdmHit or na(iCeilIdmP))

     Read it as: a CHOCH is never gated; with ALTERNATE off nothing is gated;
     otherwise the IDM must have been taken, or there must be no IDM to take.
     This is the main engine's condition transcribed one tier down - same shape,
     same order, same escape clauses.

  b) The sweep re-anchor. When ALTERNATE is on and price closes beyond the level
     without the IDM having been taken, the main engine rejects the break AND
     moves the level up to the sweep extreme, then re-arms the inducement there.
     Internal and inner now do the same, using the same field resets the main
     engine's mkSLine() performs: level and bar move to the sweep, born moves to
     this bar, the sweep and reference fields are cleared, and f_intArmIdm()
     re-arms the IDM on the new pivot.

WITH ALTERNATE OFF, all of this is dead code: "not altMode" short-circuits the
gate to true, and the re-anchor block never runs. The default chart is
untouched.

NOT CHANGED: the multi-timeframe engine (f_tfPack) still ignores ALTERNATE, for
every tier, exactly as it always has - it has never modelled inducement at all.
It stays self-consistent; it is simply a trend reading, not a mark-for-mark
replica. Teaching it IDM would have meant eight more inlined copies of that
machinery and the compiled-token limit is not far enough away to spend it there.

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A TradingView alert does not run your indicator the way your chart does. The chart
gets a browser tab with the whole machine behind it. The alert gets a slot on a
shared server that has to hold your script in memory for weeks and re-run it on
every tick. When a script asks that server for too much, the alert does not warn
you — it dies with "Calculation error" and stops sending, which is exactly what
happened to the 1-minute alert created on 1 September: it survived three days and
roughly 30-40 messages, then stopped.

The heavy part was never the market-structure engine. It was everything bolted
around it:

  1. request.security_lower_tf(...)  — the intrabar feed the volume profiles need.
     On a 1m chart the auto intrabar timeframe resolves to "1", so the script pulls
     a full second dataset of 1-minute bars in order to split 1-minute candles.
     It is a whole extra dataset for exactly one sample per candle. Pure cost,
     zero benefit, and it is the single most expensive thing in the file.

  2. Eight request.security(...) datasets for the multi-timeframe table.

  3. Hundreds of boxes and lines for the profiles, the killzones and the VWAP
     bands, all rebuilt on the last bar of every tick.

This file removes all of it and keeps the engine and the alerts.

THE MULTI-TIMEFRAME ALERTS — HOW THEY WORK NOW
================================================================================
In v16.3 the eight higher-timeframe requests were gated on the table being
visible. There is no table here, so they are gated on the alerts instead:

    a timeframe is requested  ==  it is ticked in group 8
                              AND at least one of the three
                                  "MTF alert — ..." switches is on

All three switches are off by default, so a fresh copy of this indicator makes
ZERO higher-timeframe requests. That is the state you want for a long-running
alert. Turn on only what you actually need, and untick the timeframes you do not
watch — every ticked timeframe that passes the ceiling costs one request.

The ceiling rule is unchanged from v16.3:

    chart 1m to 5m   ->  up to 4h
    chart 15m        ->  up to 1W
    chart 10m to 1h  ->  up to 1D
    chart 2h and up  ->  up to 1M

A timeframe below the chart's own is never requested. A ticked timeframe above
the ceiling is skipped in silence, because the higher-timeframe history a fast
chart is given is too short to build a structure you would trust.

A higher timeframe reports at ITS OWN close. A 4h break watched from a 15m chart
arrives when the 4h candle closes. That is correct behaviour, not a delay.

================================================================================
HOW TO USE IT
================================================================================
  1. Add this indicator to the chart alongside — or instead of — v16.3.
  2. Tick the events you want in the Alerts group.
  3. Create ONE alert, condition = this indicator, "Any alert() function call".
  4. Set "Stop after N alert triggers" if you want the alert to go quiet after a
     set number of messages instead of running until you stop it. 0 = unlimited.

Run your visual copy (v16.3, with the profiles and the table) on the chart, and
run THIS one for the alert. They are the same engine, so they agree on every
break; they just cost very different amounts to keep alive.

================================================================================
IF AN ALERT STILL DIES
================================================================================
Work down this list — each step removes real cost:

  1. Turn all three MTF alert switches off. That is eight requests gone.
  2. Turn the EMA master off.
  3. Drop "Max history to draw" further, to 150.
  4. Move the alert off the 1-minute chart. A 1m alert re-runs the engine sixty
     times more often than a 1h one for the same structure.
  5. Re-create the alert. TradingView does not restart a dead one by itself, and
     an alert that has already errored stays errored.
