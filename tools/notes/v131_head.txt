1. GOING TO v13.1 - WHAT YOU HAVE TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRADINGVIEW
  The new settings sit in the MIDDLE of the list (end of section 4), and
  TradingView remembers saved settings by their PLACE in the list. So, while
  you are FLAT (no open trade):
    a. Write down your v13.0 settings (or take screenshots).
    b. Pine Editor -> Open -> New strategy, paste
       SMC_Structure_Strategy_v13.1.txt, save it, add it to the chart.
       (If you paste over v13.0 instead: Settings -> Defaults -> Reset
       settings, then type your settings again.)
    c. Type your settings again - every old setting is in the same place
       as in v13.0; only section 4 has new boxes at its end.
    d. Delete the old alert and create a new one on v13.1: condition
       "Order fills only", message ONLY {{strategy.order.alert_message}}.
    e. Remove v13.0 from the chart (two strategies = two sets of orders).

MT5 EA
  The setting NAMES did not change, so a saved .set file still loads; the
  new settings start OFF. Compile SMC_Structure_EA_v13.1.mq5 in MetaEditor
  (F7), attach it with the same magic number, remove v13.0. Run only ONE
  copy of the EA per chart and per magic number.

4. SKIP IF THE STOP IS CLOSER THAN X % OF THE PRICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A second minimum stop, always in % of the entry price, whatever unit
section 1 uses - so your stop buffer can stay in price (1.0).
   X = 0.5, gold at 4,000: the stop must be at least 20.00 away.
   X = 0.5, gold at 2,000: at least 10.00.
A closer stop is skipped ("CANCELLED - stop closer than 0.5% of the
price"). It is checked where the other stop-distance limits are checked.
The older box at the start of section 4 ("Skip the setup if the stop is
CLOSER than") still works too, in the section 1 unit.
Why: very small stops lose most often, and the costs are a bigger part of
a small stop. 0.5 alone took your result from -6,731 to about 0 in the
tests, and cut the trades by two thirds.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SKIP THE FIRST SETUP OF THE DAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first setup of each day that would really be taken is skipped; the
next ones are taken. The day = the calendar day of the session timezone
(section 9, India by default).
   Setups at 07:10, 09:30 and 14:00  ->  07:10 skipped, the others taken.
A setup that another filter skips (or that is not taken for any other
reason - outside the hours, already in a trade ...) does not count as the
first. In the tests the first trade of the day was the worst one.
Use it on the 1, 5 and 15-minute charts. On a 1-hour or bigger chart there
is rarely more than one setup a day, so it would skip almost all of them.
EA: the day whose first setup was used is kept over a restart. On the day
you START the EA it does not know the setups before it started, so the
first one it sees that day counts as the first (TradingView knows the
whole history).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. NO BUY BELOW YESTERDAY'S LOW, NO SELL ABOVE YESTERDAY'S HIGH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A buy is skipped when the signal candle CLOSES below yesterday's low; a
sell when it closes above yesterday's high - the price already broke out
of yesterday's range the other way.
   Yesterday 4,080 - 4,120:  buy at 4,070 skipped, buy at 4,095 taken,
                             sell at 4,130 skipped, sell at 4,100 taken.
"Yesterday" = the gold trading day that ended at 17:00 New York (02:30
IST in US summer time, 03:30 IST in winter): the gold daily close. After a
weekend, yesterday is Friday. Alone: -6,731 -> -1,777 in the tests.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SKIP A CHOCH / BOS WHOSE MOVE TOOK FEWER THAN N CANDLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Counts the candles from the swing point that becomes your stop level (the
low for a buy, the high for a sell - the CHOCH* level) to the candle that
broke the structure. A faster move - a spike, often news - is skipped.
   N = 12, 1-minute chart, swing low at 10:00:
     break at 10:05 = 5 candles   -> skipped
     break at 10:20 = 20 candles  -> taken
On a 15-minute chart 12 candles = 3 hours - choose N for your chart.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. TRADE ONLY WITH TODAY'S MOVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A buy only when the signal candle closes ABOVE today's open by at least
X x the average daily range; a sell only BELOW it. The average daily range
= the average of (high - low) of the last 14 complete days. Today starts at
17:00 New York, like "yesterday" above.
   X = 0.15, today's open 4,000, average day 40.00:
     buys only above 4,006.00, sells only below 3,994.00.
   X = 0: buys only above today's open, sells only below it.
Until 14 complete days of history exist, it lets setups through.
EA: with this filter ON the EA reads at least 17 days of chart history at
the start (more than the 20,000 bars of section 12 on a 1-minute chart).
It was part of the C4 set in the tests (not C5).

9. RSI FILTERS - A LOWER AND A HIGHER TIMEFRAME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two separate RSI filters; each has its own switch, timeframe, length, rule
and level. Use one, the other, or both.
  TIMEFRAME: 1 minute, 5 minutes, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours, 1 day,
  1 week, 1 month (your list - nothing else).
  A timeframe ABOVE the chart reads its last CLOSED candle (the one
  before the candle that holds the signal), so the backtest and live
  trading agree. A timeframe NOT above the chart (1 minute on a 1-minute
  or a 15-minute chart) uses the chart's own candles, including the
  signal candle.
  RULE "Pullback": a buy needs RSI BELOW the level, a sell needs RSI ABOVE
  100 minus the level.
     level 50: buy below 50, sell above 50
     level 30: buy below 30, sell above 70 (only deep dips)
  RULE "Momentum": a buy needs RSI ABOVE the level, a sell BELOW 100 minus
  it.  level 55: buy above 55, sell below 45.
  LENGTH: 2 = very fast (the tested one), 14 = the classic RSI.
Tested: HIGHER timeframe 1 hour, length 2, pullback, level 50 = take a
buy only when the last closed 1-hour candle has RSI(2) below 50 - gold has
just dipped on the 1-hour chart.
Careful: on the chart's own timeframe a CHOCH up candle almost always has
RSI above 50 (it broke up), so the PULLBACK rule there blocks nearly every
signal. On the chart timeframe use the momentum rule, or a higher
timeframe.
The RSI is TradingView's own RSI (Wilder's averages).

10. EMA FILTERS - A LOWER AND A HIGHER TIMEFRAME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two separate EMA filters, each with its own switch, timeframe (the same
8 as the RSI: 1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 1D, 1W, 1M), rule and lengths.
  RULE "Price above the EMA": a buy needs the signal candle's close ABOVE
  the EMA, a sell BELOW it.
     EMA 200 = the classic trend line, 50 / 100 = medium, 9 / 20 = short.
     Example: 1 hour, EMA 200 at 4,012: a buy at 4,020 is taken, a buy at
     4,005 is skipped.
  RULE "Fast EMA above the slow EMA": a buy needs the FAST EMA above the
  SLOW one (for example 9 above 20, or 50 above 200), a sell below it. The
  fast one must be shorter than the slow one.
     Example: 1 hour 50 / 200: buys only while the 1-hour EMA 50 is above
     the 1-hour EMA 200 (the "golden cross" side).
  Lengths 1 - 500.

12. TRADINGVIEW AND THE EA - SMALL DIFFERENCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - 4-hour, 1-day, 1-week and 1-month RSI / EMA: TradingView (OANDA)
    starts those candles at 17:00 New York; FxPro MT5 candles follow the
    broker clock (European summer time). For a few weeks a year (March,
    October / November) the two clocks are one hour apart, so a candle can
    differ a little. 1 hour and below: the same candles.
  - 1 week / 1 month: the EA needs that much history in MT5 (it reads up
    to 3,000 candles of the filter's timeframe; open the W1 / MN chart of
    the symbol once if the history is short). An EMA 200 on the monthly
    chart needs 200 months - use short lengths there (e.g. EMA 6 or 12).
  - At the very start of the history, while an RSI / EMA does not have
    enough candles yet, TradingView and the EA can differ for a moment
    (the filter lets setups through until it has a value). The EA reads
    3,000 candles of the filter's timeframe before it starts, so live it
    always has a value.
  - "Skip the first setup of the day" on the day the EA starts: see 5.
