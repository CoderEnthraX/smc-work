================================================================================
SMC STRUCTURE STRATEGY  -  v2.2
How the whole thing works, in plain English
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
This is a trading strategy built on the same market-structure engine as your
v17.3 indicator. 48 of its 49 engine functions are byte-for-byte identical to
v17.3, so the CHOCH and BOS it trades are the same ones your chart draws. There
is no second engine and nothing can drift apart.

It runs on whatever chart timeframe you put it on. It watches the MAIN structure
tier only - iBOS / iCHOCH / nBOS / nCHOCH are computed but not traded.

================================================================================
1. WHEN IT DECIDES TO TRADE
================================================================================
A trade starts on a main CHOCH. If you switch on "ALSO take trades on BOS", a
main BOS starts one too. Bullish signal means a long, bearish means a short.
Every signal is confirmed at the CLOSE of the bar - nothing repaints.

Then it picks ONE of two rules, based on what a higher timeframe is doing.

RULE 2 - used when the higher timeframe AGREES
    If the higher timeframe's main trend is bullish and you just got a bullish
    signal (or both bearish), the order goes on the PIVOT THE CANDLE JUST BROKE.
    For a bullish signal that pivot is below price, so it is a buy limit sitting
    under the market waiting for a retest.

RULE 1 - used when the higher timeframe DISAGREES
    The order goes at a percentage pullback instead. The pullback is measured
    from the live CHOCH* level up to the highest point the move has reached, and
    50% means halfway between them.

    It RATCHETS. Every new high pushes the level up and the waiting order moves
    with it. A candle against the move leaves it exactly where it was. So you
    are always buying a retrace of the move AS IT STANDS RIGHT NOW, not of a
    move that finished ten candles ago.

WHICH HIGHER TIMEFRAME - the pairing
    1m chart  -> 15m        1h chart -> 1D
    5m chart  -> 1h         4h chart -> 1W
    15m chart -> 4h         1D chart -> 1M
    "Auto" does this for you. Or pick one by hand. If what you pick is not ABOVE
    the chart timeframe it is ignored and everything falls through to rule 1 -
    you cannot build a higher-timeframe filter out of a faster timeframe.

================================================================================
2. THE STOP AND THE TARGET
================================================================================
STOP    the live CHOCH* level - the price that would flip the structure back
        the other way - plus an optional buffer so a wick that just kisses the
        level does not take you out.
        While the order is still WAITING the stop follows that level as the
        engine moves it. The moment the order fills the stop LOCKS and never
        moves again.
TARGET  a multiple of the distance from your entry to your stop. 1.0 is the
        classic 1:1. Set 2, 3, 5 or anything between.

================================================================================
3. HOW BIG THE TRADE IS  -  and why a money stop works on every ticker
================================================================================
Default mode is RISK-BASED. You say "risk 50" and it works out the rest:

    how much does ONE unit lose if my stop is hit, in the symbol's currency
      -> convert that into the account currency
      -> buy exactly enough units that the stop costs 50

That currency conversion is the piece that makes 50 mean the same 50 on XAUUSD,
EURUSD, USDJPY or an index. You never type a lot size.

CHECK IT YOURSELF. The table has a "Last trade risk" row showing what the filled
trade ACTUALLY had at risk, worked out from the real fill size and the real stop.
It should read 50.

MAX LEVERAGE matters more than it looks. A tight stop means a BIG position - a
10-point stop on gold with 50 risk is about 21,000 of position. TradingView will
not open a position bigger than the account allows, and it does not tell you, it
just drops the order. That is what limited an early version to two trades in a
fortnight. The leverage setting caps the position at equity x leverage; 30 is
normal for retail gold and FX. Watch the "Size capped" row.

Two other sizing modes exist if you prefer: fixed quantity, and fixed cash.

================================================================================
4. WHEN IT IS ALLOWED TO TRADE
================================================================================
All times are in your chosen timezone, Asia/Kolkata (IST) by default.

    Entries taken         06:00 to 23:00
    Force-flat            02:00 - any open trade is closed at market
    Weekend               closes everything and stops from Friday 21:00

An open trade is NOT closed at 23:00. Only NEW entries stop, and any waiting
order is cancelled.

The force-flat does not need a bar at exactly 02:00. It fires on the first bar in
the dead zone between the flat hour and the open hour, so a Monday reopen at
03:30 still flattens. That matters - the old version needed a bar in that exact
hour, and a weekend or broker break meant it never ran.

================================================================================
5. THE SAFETY GUARDS  -  group 24, all optional
================================================================================
CANCEL ON ANY NEW SIGNAL (on by default)
    The moment a new CHOCH or BOS prints, a waiting order that has not filled is
    pulled - even if the new signal does not itself produce a tradeable setup.
    Off, the old order only goes when the new signal actually arms something, so
    a rejected signal leaves a stale order sitting against a market that has
    already moved on.

CANCEL AFTER N BARS
    A setup that has waited this long without filling is abandoned.

SKIP IF THE STOP IS TOO CLOSE / TOO FAR
    Too close: a 2-point stop on 1m gold is inside the spread, and risk-based
    sizing answers it by buying an enormous position. Try 3 on gold.
    Too far: a very wide stop makes the position tiny and the target unreachable
    in the time you have.

MAX TRADES PER DAY - a brake on a choppy day that keeps handing out signals.

STOP FOR THE DAY AFTER LOSING X - the most useful guard here. Set it before you
    go live. It adds up closed profit and loss for the day, stops when the day is
    down by that much, and starts fresh tomorrow.

STOP PERMANENTLY AFTER AN EQUITY DRAWDOWN OF X% - the last line of defence,
    measured from the highest equity reached. This one does not reset.

WEEKEND FLAT - closes and stops from your cutoff until the market reopens.
    Without it a Friday-night trade carries the weekend gap, and a gap does not
    respect your stop, it jumps straight past it.

The "BLOCKED NOW" row in the table tells you which of these is stopping trading
at this moment, if any.

================================================================================
6. THE TWO LOSS-RECOVERY MODES  -  both OFF, and please read the warning
================================================================================
    RULE A   50 -> 100 -> 200 -> 400 ...   next risk = losses so far + base
    RULE B   50 -> 100 -> 300 -> 900 ...   next risk = 2 x losses so far
A win resets either to the base. Rule A is written as (losses + base) / RR so the
WIN nets the base amount whatever target multiple you use.

### THE WARNING ###
Both of these make you BIGGER after losing. That is the most reliable way there
is to empty an account. Rule B, uncapped, from a base of 50:

    50 -> 100 -> 300 -> 900 -> 2700 -> 8100 -> 24300 -> 72900

Seven losers in a row asks for 24,300 of risk. Seven in a row is not rare - at a
45% win rate it happens about once in every 60 trades, and this strategy can take
several trades a day. The sequence does not know or care how big your account is.

That is why the HARD CAP defaults to 500 and "when the cap is reached" defaults
to "stop for the rest of the day". Please do not remove the cap to let it run -
the cap is the thing keeping the account alive. Backtest both against plain fixed
risk over a long window before you trust either.

================================================================================
7. THE COUNTER TABLE  -  what each row means
================================================================================
CHOCH signals / BOS signals   how many the strategy saw
Setups armed                  signals that passed the rules and got an order
Orders FILLED                 those orders that actually traded
Armed, never filled           placed, then cancelled or replaced before price
                              reached them
Exit - target / stop          finished on its own bracket
Exit - opposite sig           cut short by the opposite signal
Exit - time flat              closed by the force-flat or the weekend cutoff
Size capped (leverage)        the leverage cap shrank the position
Risk NEXT trade               what the next trade will risk
Last trade risk               what the last filled trade ACTUALLY risked
Losses carried                the recovery sequence's running loss
Risk cap hit / HALT           recovery cap episodes, and whether it is halted
RULE 2 timeframe              which higher timeframe is actually in use
Skipped - stop too tight/wide the stop-distance guard rejected it
Expired unfilled              the N-bar expiry cancelled it
BLOCKED NOW                   what is stopping trading right now

HOW TO READ IT
  armed large, filled small     -> your pullback % is too deep
  target and stop both small
  while opposite sig is large   -> trades are cut short before the R target is
                                   ever tested; try turning off close-and-reverse
  size capped climbing          -> raise max leverage
  ARMED always equals FILLED + NEVER FILLED + the one setup that may be live
  right now. That identity was checked over 600,000 simulated bars.

8. THE SETTINGS PANEL
================================================================================
239 of the old 269 settings were turned into fixed values. They are still in the
code and the structure engine still uses them - they simply do not clutter the
panel any more. What is left:

  20 Strategy          the two rules, pullback %, target, session hours, entry
                       order handling, drawing, table
  21 Position size     sizing mode, base risk, max leverage, recovery modes
  22 Webhook           message on/off, format, licence ID
  23 Engine rules      ALTERNATE, and pullback rules 1/2/3 - these change the
                       SIGNALS themselves, which is why they stayed visible
  24 Safety            everything in section 5

ENTRY ORDER HANDLING - two modes, pick either
  CONTINUOUS   the order is re-placed each bar at the recomputed level, so a
               rule-1 entry ratchets with the move
  SEND ONCE    the level is worked out once when the setup arms and the order is
               left exactly there. The stop freezes too, so what was placed and
               what is tracked cannot drift apart.
  Neither changes how many webhook messages you get - see below.

9. CONNECTING A BROKER
================================================================================
TradingView's built-in broker panel CANNOT auto-trade a Pine strategy. Even with
a broker connected, that panel is for clicking by hand. Automation only happens
through alerts and webhooks.

A STRATEGY ALERT FIRES WHEN AN ORDER FILLS, not when it is placed. Re-placing a
resting limit sends nothing to your bridge. (I told you the opposite once - that
was wrong.)

FOUR ROUTES
  1. Webhook -> bridge -> MT5 / cTrader. What you want. Third-party, paid.
  2. Webhook -> your own script using the MT5 Python API or cTrader Open API.
     Most control, most work.
  3. Crypto-only services connect directly. Not relevant for gold or FX.
  4. Semi-auto: alerts to your phone, you click. Honestly the best first step.

DEMO STEPS
  1. A paid TradingView plan - webhooks are not on Free.
  2. An MT5 or cTrader DEMO account with your broker.
  3. A bridge, in demo/paper mode.
  4. In group 22: tick the webhook switch, pick the format, paste your ID.
  5. ONE alert on the strategy, condition = the strategy, "Any alert() function
     call", paste the bridge's webhook URL.
  6. Run it 2-4 weeks on demo before any real money.

10. WHAT IT WILL NOT DO, AND WHAT TO WATCH
================================================================================
  . A Pine strategy holds ONE net position. It cannot run a long and a short at
    once, each with its own stop. That is why "take the opposite trade as well"
    became close-and-reverse.
  . close_all runs at the NEXT bar's open. Reversals, the time-flat and the
    weekend flat are one bar late by design.
  . The leverage cap is in the script, not at your broker. Theirs may be lower
    and will reject what the backtest happily took.
  . Set COMMISSION and SLIPPAGE in the Properties tab and turn on BAR MAGNIFIER.
    All three default to the flattering setting. Without the magnifier, when one
    bar covers both your stop and your target TradingView assumes the stop - so
    the reported win rate is pessimistic, not optimistic.
  . Spread and latency are real. Bar close -> alert -> bridge -> broker is
    typically 1-5 seconds, and gold's spread is 20-40 cents.

================================================================================
11. EVERY BUG FOUND ALONG THE WAY
================================================================================
Recorded because each one changes how you should read the older backtests.

  v1.1  25 alertcondition() calls did nothing inside a strategy; the arm-status
        table belonged to the alert file. Removed.
  v1.2  Before release: a same-direction signal while in a trade armed a stale
        setup that fired the instant the trade closed. And because the pending
        stop follows the live level, that level could move past a resting order
        and leave the stop on the WRONG SIDE of the entry - the order already
        resting stayed live. Both fixed.
  v1.3  THE BIG ONE. Risk-based sizing asked for positions bigger than the
        account, and TradingView silently discarded them. 131 of 133 setups were
        binned; only the three widest stops in a fortnight produced a position
        small enough to fit. Fixed with margin 0 plus an explicit leverage cap.
  v2.1  A permanent deadlock: "stop when the cap is reached" set a halt flag
        cleared only by a WINNING trade - but the halt blocked all entries, so no
        trade could open, so none could win. Once tripped it was dead for good.
        Also: the force-flat needed a bar in the exact flat hour, so a weekend or
        broker break meant it never ran.
  v2.2  Caught before release: the day-trade counter was declared AFTER the
        block that increments it, which would not have compiled.

================================================================================
12. HOW THIS VERSION WAS CHECKED
================================================================================
  . The whole order state machine was rebuilt outside Pine and replayed over
    480,000 bars in FIVE configurations - continuous and send-once, with and
    without cancel-on-signal, with the expiry and with the stop-distance band.
    Five invariants on every bar: never both sides resting, never a pending
    setup surviving a fill, never in a position without a locked stop, and the
    bracket always on the correct side of the fill for both directions.
    ZERO violations in all five.
  . The counter identity - armed = filled + never-filled + one possibly live -
    balanced exactly in all five.
  . Send-once provably places at most one order per setup.
  . 48 of 49 engine functions byte-identical to v17.3; the 49th differs only in
    lines this file never reads.
  . Pine's own build-time input validation, an undefined-identifier scan, a
    use-before-declare scan, indentation, brackets, empty blocks, duplicate
    declarations, table bounds - all clean. Pure 7-bit ASCII throughout.
