WHAT v7.2 CHANGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
48. EACH STOP OR TARGET CLOSES ONLY ITS OWN TRADE (bug fix for v7.1).
    With several trades open, TradingView's default rule is "first in,
    first out": whichever trade's stop or target is hit, it closes the
    OLDEST trade first. With stacked trades of different sizes that
    closed the wrong trade and cut trades into two pieces in the List
    of Trades - in the simulator about 1 exit in 7 to 1 in 9 with BOS
    on. Live, FxPro closes the right trade by its tag, so the backtest
    did not match live. v7.2 tells TradingView to close the trade each
    stop or target belongs to. "CHOCH only" was never affected: it
    holds one trade at a time.

49. A TRADE CODE THAT DOES NOT CHANGE. v7.1 numbered trades 1, 2, 3...
    from the first candle on the chart. When TradingView restarts an
    alert, it starts again from a different first candle, so the numbers
    move, and a close could carry another tag than the trade open at
    FxPro. The code is now made from the signal candle's time - 4
    letters and digits, e.g. smcgold-k3f9. The same candle always gives
    the same code.

50. THE TABLE. "Trades entered 312  (3 still open)", and a new row under
    it: "TradingView List of Trades  309 closed + 3 open". Orange when
    the two do not add up to the same number. See "COUNTING TRADES".

51. A NEW ENTRY IS FOUND BY ITS ORDER ID. The count was already right in
    every simulated run; this makes it independent of the order in which
    TradingView lists trades that closed on the same candle.

WHAT v4.1 FIXED  -  the counter table
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. "ORDERS FILLED" COUNTED MARGIN-CALL SLICES AS NEW TRADES.
    Your table said 54 setups armed and 100 orders filled. That is
    impossible - every trade starts from an armed setup - and it was a bug.

    What happened: when the Properties tab's leverage is low enough (yours
    was 30x), TradingView simulates a broker margin call. It does not close the
    whole position. It closes a SLICE of it, and every slice appears as a
    separate closed trade in the List of Trades. Your screenshot shows it:
    one trade of 75.8 and one of 0.2 with the same entry - that is one
    76-unit (0.76 lot) trade cut in two. v4.0 detected a new entry by
    watching TradingView's trade count go up, so every slice looked like a
    new fill. The same slices were then counted as extra stop-outs, and
    they counted toward "Max trades per day".

    Fixed: a new entry is now detected by its ENTRY BAR. A slice has the
    same entry bar as the trade it was cut from, so it can never be counted
    as a new trade again. Slices get their own row, "Margin calls
    (Properties)", instead of being mixed into the stop count.

    What it changes in trading: only one thing. If margin calls happen AND
    "Max trades per day" is on, v4.0 counted slices toward the daily
    maximum and stopped trading too early. v4.1 counts real trades only.
    In every other case v4.1 takes exactly the same trades as v4.0 -
    checked in the simulator (see "HOW v4.1 WAS CHECKED").

13. "SIZE CAPPED" COUNTED WAITING BARS, NOT TRADES.
    It was 21 in continuous mode and 10 in send-once mode on the same
    chart. The old row counted every SETUP that touched the leverage cap
    on ANY bar while it waited. In continuous mode the order is re-sized
    on every bar, so a setup that brushed the cap once and then filled
    below it - or never filled at all - still counted. Send-once sizes the
    order once, so it counted less.

    Fixed: the row is now "Trades cut by leverage cap" and counts TRADES
    that were actually entered at a size the cap had cut. Now both modes
    count the same thing, and the number can never be larger than
    "Trades entered".

14. RULE 1 / RULE 2 COUNTS WERE MISSING.
    New rows under "Trades entered": how many trades were entered by
    ENTRY RULE 1 (pullback %) and how many by ENTRY RULE 2 (broken pivot).
    The two always add up to "Trades entered".

15. "ENTRY RULES" LOOKED LIKE THE LOSS RECOVERY SETTING.
    You had Loss recovery set to Rule B and the table said "Entry rules
    1 + 2 (dynamic)". Nothing was wrong - these are two DIFFERENT settings
    that both happen to use the word "rule":

      ENTRY RULE 1 / 2    HOW a trade is entered (pullback %, or the broken
                          pivot when the higher timeframe agrees). Group 20.
      LOSS RECOVERY A / B HOW MUCH the next trade risks after a loss. Group 21.

    The table now shows both, each with its full name: "ENTRY rules in use
    (1 / 2)" and "LOSS RECOVERY (A / B)". Yours will read "1 + 2 (dynamic)"
    and "B - double the losses", which is correct for your settings.

16. "RULE 2 TIMEFRAME" SHOWED A BARE "15".
    TradingView names timeframes in minutes, so 15 minutes is "15". The row
    now prints 15m, 1h, 4h. It also says why rule 2 is off when it is off.

17. SIGNAL ROWS NOW COUNT EVERY SIGNAL ON THE CHART.
    "CHOCH signals" and "BOS signals" used to count only the signals seen
    while trading was allowed - a signal during a news window, a weekend
    block, a daily-loss halt or a risk-cap halt was left out. They now
    count every main-tier CHOCH / BOS on the chart (of the kinds you have

None of these touch the structure engine, the entry rules, the stop, the
target, the sizing or the webhook messages.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS STRATEGY IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
It is the SMC Market Structure indicator (v17.3 engine) with a trading
layer bolted on top. The engine finds market structure exactly as the
indicator does - the same pivots, the same 3-candle pullback, the same
BOS and CHOCH. The trading layer watches only the MAIN tier (not internal,
not inner) and turns those events into orders.

Nothing about the structure engine was changed in v2.3. It is byte for byte
the same as the indicator you already use. Only the strategy block changed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE v4.0 AUDIT FOUND  -  and what each fix means for you
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Eleven bugs. Listed roughly in order of how much money they could cost.

 1. TRADES THE STRATEGY NEVER SAW, AND TOOK TWICE.
    When an entry filled AND hit its stop or target inside the same bar,
    the position was already flat by the time the strategy looked. It
    never registered the trade. The setup stayed armed, so in continuous
    mode it put the same order back and could take the SAME signal a
    second time. "Max trades per day" also undercounted. In simulation
    this was 4.5% of all trades on 1-minute gold. Fixed: fills are now
    counted from TradingView's own trade list, which cannot miss one.
    (v4.1 refines how: see fix 12 - margin-call slices - under
    "WHAT v4.1 FIXED".)

 2. RULE B COULD STOP TRADING FOREVER.
    After a partial win left a small debt - even a floating-point crumb
    like 0.00000000001 - Rule B risked "double the debt", which is far
    below the smallest lot. The order rounded to nothing, the setup was
    skipped, and since no trade could happen, the debt could never be
    paid. Simulated: an account owing 0.30 under Rule B took ZERO trades
    in two weeks. Fixed: debt under half a cent counts as paid, and
    Rule B never risks less than your base amount. Your sequences
    (50 -> 100 -> 300 -> 900) are unchanged.

 3. COMMISSION WAS COUNTED TWICE.
    TradingView's per-trade profit already has the Properties commission
    taken off. v3.x took it off again. Once you set commission in
    Properties - as these notes told you to - every trade looked worse
    than it was: the daily loss limit tripped early and the recovery
    ladder sized up for losses that did not exist. With a 120 daily loss
    limit, v3.3 took 31 trades in a test where the correct count was 45.
    (The v3.x notes said "it never adds both". That was wrong. It did.)

 4. THE HIGHER TIMEFRAME REPAINTED LIVE.
    Rule 1 versus rule 2 is decided by the higher timeframe's trend. In
    the backtest that came from CLOSED higher-timeframe bars; live, it
    came from the bar still forming - so the same signal could take a
    different rule live than it did in your backtest. Fixed: the trend
    now always comes from the last closed higher-timeframe bar, the
    standard TradingView method, so live and backtest see the same value.

 5. EVERY TIME RULE FIRED ONE BAR LATE.
    Session start and end, the 02:00 force-close, the weekend cutoff and
    the news windows were all judged at the last instant INSIDE a bar
    instead of the instant the strategy actually acts. On 1m that was a
    minute. On 1h the force-close happened at 03:00, not 02:00. On 4h
    the weekend flatten came three hours after your cutoff, and a news
    window on 4h never fired at all. Fixed - see the timing table in
    "TIME-OF-DAY FILTERS".

 6. THE BROKER'S TARGET AND TRADINGVIEW'S TARGET COULD DRIFT APART.
    The target sent to FxPro was fixed at entry, but TradingView kept
    recalculating its own copy every bar from the actual fill price. On a
    gapped fill, or in percent-commission mode, TradingView then exited
    at a different price than your broker. Fixed: the bracket is frozen
    at fill to exactly the values your broker was sent.

 7. A HALT BLOCKED THE PROTECTIVE EXIT.
    With the daily loss limit, max trades, the risk cap or the drawdown
    stop active, an opposite signal would NOT close a running trade -
    even though outside session hours it would. A halt is meant to stop
    new risk, not to stop you getting out. Fixed: the opposite-signal
    exit now works whenever it is switched on.

 8. A PENDING SETUP COULD SURVIVE A TREND FLIP.
    The rule-1 pullback level is worked out from the main trend. If the
    trend flipped against a pending setup (easiest in "BOS only" mode),
    a pending BUY was re-priced from the new DOWN leg - a sell-side level
    used as a buy price. Fixed: a pending setup is cancelled the moment
    the trend turns against it.

 9. A STALE SETUP COULD WAKE UP THE NEXT DAY.
    A setup armed on the same bar the daily loss limit was hit stayed
    armed, and with the time filters off it was placed the next morning
    from yesterday's levels. Fixed: while entries are blocked, nothing
    stays armed.

10. THE STRATEGY STILL FIRED THE INDICATOR'S OWN ALERTS.
    Inherited from the indicator: every BOS and CHOCH also sent a text
    alert like "Bullish BOS - XAUUSD - 1 - 4312.5". If your TradingView
    alert was set to "Order fills and alert() function calls" - one of
    the three options in the dropdown - those texts went to your broker
    bridge next to the real orders. Fixed: switched off. Now either
    alert option is safe.

11. TINY ORDERS COULD BE SENT AS "qty 0".
    With lot rounding off, a near-empty position was printed in the
    webhook as zero. Fixed: the message carries 8 decimals, and an order
    too small to express is never placed.

None of these changed how structure is found. The BOS/CHOCH engine is the
same engine as the v17.3 indicator, line for line - the only edits in that
part of the file switch off bug 10's alerts.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TAKE TRADES ON  -  CHOCH only / BOS only / CHOCH and BOS      (NEW in v2.3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This decides which structure events are allowed to start a trade.

  CHOCH only        A change of character. This is the reversal signal -
                    price has broken the other way for the first time.
                    Fewest trades. This is the default.

  BOS only          A break of structure. This is the continuation signal -
                    price keeps going the way it was already going.
                    No reversal entries at all.

  CHOCH and BOS     Both. Several times as many signals.

Everything downstream is identical in all three. Same two entry rules, same
stop, same target, same sizing, same safety guards. The only thing this
setting changes is which events the strategy is allowed to look at.

Important: events you switch OFF do not arm a setup, do not reverse a
running trade, and do not cancel a resting order. If you choose "CHOCH
only", a BOS printing on the chart does nothing at all. That is
deliberate - if you are not trading BOS, a BOS is not news.

v7.1, two things on top: with BOS switched on, a BOS the same way as your
open trades ADDS a trade of its own; and an OPPOSITE CHOCH closes every
open trade in all three modes - BOS only included. See "EXTRA TRADES ON
BOS".

In v2.2 this was a single on/off tick box called "ALSO take trades on BOS",
which could not express "BOS only". It is now a proper three-way choice.
Because the setting changed type, TradingView cannot carry your old value
across - check this setting after you load v2.3.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 1  -  PULLBACK % ENTRY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
When a signal fires, the strategy does not buy at market. It works out a
retracement level of the leg and leaves a limit order there.

The level is measured from the live CHOCH* level up to the running extreme
of the leg, at whatever percentage you set (default 50%).

It RATCHETS. Every time the leg makes a new extreme, the level moves and
the resting order moves with it. A candle that goes against the leg leaves
it alone. So the order chases the move up but never backs away from it.

  Rule 1 alone (rule 2 off)   Every signal is entered this way, whatever
                              the higher timeframe is doing. The higher
                              timeframe is not even requested, so the
                              script also loads faster.

  Rule 1 + rule 2             Rule 1 handles the signals where the higher
                              timeframe DISAGREES.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 2  -  ENTRY AT THE BROKEN PIVOT, HIGHER TIMEFRAME MUST AGREE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
When the higher timeframe trend points the same way as the signal, the
strategy does not wait for a percentage pullback. It puts the limit order
at the pivot the signal just broke - the level price has just cleared.

The idea: when the bigger picture agrees, you do not need the discount. A
retest of the broken level is enough.

  Rule 2 alone (rule 1 off)   A signal is taken ONLY when the higher
                              timeframe agrees. Everything else is skipped
                              entirely - no trade at all.

  Rule 1 + rule 2             The strategy decides per signal. Higher
                              timeframe agrees -> rule 2. Disagrees ->
                              rule 1. This is the default and it is fully
                              dynamic, signal by signal.

  Both off                    No trades at all. The table shows
                              "Entry rules: NONE - both off" in red so you
                              cannot leave it like that by accident.

If rule 2 is on but the broken pivot cannot be identified for that signal,
the setup is skipped rather than guessed at.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
HIGHER TIMEFRAME FOR RULE 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Auto pairs it with whatever chart you are on:

    1m chart   ->  15m
    5m chart   ->  1h
    15m chart  ->  4h
    1h chart   ->  1D
    4h chart   ->  1W
    1D chart   ->  1M

Or pick one by hand from the list. If the timeframe you pick is not ABOVE
the chart timeframe it is ignored and every signal falls through to rule 1 -
you cannot build a higher-timeframe filter out of a faster timeframe. The
table row "RULE 2 timeframe" tells you which one is actually in use, or
says "off (not above chart)".

CONFIRMED BARS ONLY (v4.0). The higher timeframe's trend is taken from its
last CLOSED bar. On a 1-minute chart with a 15-minute filter, the trend that
decides rule 1 versus rule 2 is the one the last completed 15-minute bar
settled on - never the one the current 15-minute bar is still making up its
mind about. That is what makes the choice identical in your backtest and
live. The cost: a new higher-timeframe trend is picked up when its bar
closes, not while it is forming. That is the honest price of a filter that
does not change its mind after the fact.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 2 KEEPS ITS BRACKET                              (FIXED in v7.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rule 2 enters at the pivot the signal just broke. That entry price is
fixed the moment the setup is armed. Up to v6.1 its STOP was not: in
"Continuous - follow the level" mode it kept following the live CHOCH*
level, bar after bar, like rule 1's does.

That mix broke setups. Example, "CHOCH only", long:
  - CHOCH up breaks the lower high at 4310. The higher timeframe agrees:
    rule 2 arms a buy limit at 4310, stop under the swing low at 4302.
  - Price runs up without coming back, and makes a BOS up at 4325. You
    do not trade BOS, so no new setup - but the engine moves the live
    CHOCH* level up to the new swing low, 4314.
  - The stop followed it to 4314 - ABOVE the 4310 entry. A buy with its
    stop above the entry is impossible, so the setup was cancelled, and
    nothing told you.
  - Price then came back to 4310: the trade you expected never happened.

In the simulator this killed between about 1 in 12 and 1 in 4 rule-2
setups, depending on the settings, with CHOCH, BOS and both.

v7.0: rule 2 keeps the stop it was armed with - the swing low (or high)
of the signal it belongs to - until the order fills or is cancelled. The
entry and the stop now belong to the same signal. Rule 1 still follows
the level in Continuous mode, exactly as before (its entry moves with
the leg, so its stop has to move too). "Send once" mode already kept
everything fixed and is unchanged.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHICH SIGNALS BECOME TRADES  -  and every reason one does not   (v7.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RULE CHOICE, for every CHOCH / BOS allowed by "Take trades on":

  Rule 1 only    every signal -> rule 1 (pullback %). The higher
                 timeframe is not asked at all.
  Rule 2 only    higher timeframe AGREES -> rule 2 (broken pivot).
                 Disagrees or undecided -> skipped.
  Rule 1 + 2     agrees -> rule 2. Disagrees or undecided -> rule 1.

"Agrees" is judged on the last CLOSED higher-timeframe candle (15m on a
1m chart), so it never repaints. A 15m candle that is still forming can
show something different on the 15m chart - the strategy only uses it
once it has closed.

A SIGNAL MAKES NO SETUP WHEN (the label says SKIP):
  - trading is blocked: news window, US holiday, weekend, daily loss or
    trade limit, risk cap, profit target, drawdown stop;
  - it is outside the session hours (group 20) - it is not kept for
    later, the next signal inside the hours is taken;
  - you are already in a trade the same way and the signal is not a BOS
    (since v7.1 a same-side BOS adds a trade - see "EXTRA TRADES ON
    BOS"), or you are in the opposite trade with "close and reverse" OFF;
  - rule 2 only, and the higher timeframe does not agree;
  - there is no structure level on the other side for the stop.

A SETUP THAT WAS ARMED ENDS WITHOUT A TRADE WHEN (label: CANCELLED):
  - price never comes back to the entry before the next signal (the new
    signal replaces it), before the trend flips against it, or before
    the session ends, a news window starts or the weekend cutoff;
  - its stop is closer / further than your group 24 limits (with your
    3.0 minimum on gold this is the single biggest reason - in the
    simulator it removed about 1 in 3 armed CHOCH setups);
  - the size comes out below your broker's minimum lot, or zero.

Everything else is FILLED.

SIGNAL AUDIT LABELS (group 20, OFF by default). Switch it on and every
signal on the chart gets a small label, so you can see all of the above
yourself:

    CHOCH up  HTF up              first line: the signal and the higher-
    R2 ARMED                      timeframe trend the rule choice used
    FILLED                        (up / dn / - ; "off" when rule 2 is
                                  off). Then what happened.

    BOS dn  HTF -
    SKIP - already in a trade the same way

    CHOCH dn  HTF up
    R1 ARMED
    CANCELLED - stop too close / too far

Blue = armed, green = filled, orange = cancelled, grey = skipped. The
labels change no trade. TradingView keeps at most 500 labels, shared
with the structure labels, so on a long history the oldest disappear.
If you ever see a signal whose label does not explain why it was not
traded, screenshot it with the date and time and send it to me.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXTRA TRADES ON BOS  -  several trades at once              (NEW in v7.1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT HAPPENS, by "Take trades on":

  CHOCH only      Unchanged. One trade at a time - a BOS is not a signal
                  here, so it neither adds nor closes anything.

  CHOCH and BOS   You are in a trade from a CHOCH (rule 1 or rule 2). A
                  BOS prints the SAME way: the CHOCH trade stays open, and
                  the BOS gets a trade of its own - rule 1 or rule 2 by the
                  higher timeframe, exactly like any signal, with its own
                  entry, stop, target and full risk. Every further BOS the
                  same way adds one more.

  BOS only        The same: while BOS trades are open, every new BOS the
                  same way adds another.

A same-side CHOCH never adds a trade (it can only happen with "close and
reverse" OFF).

HOW TRADES END:
  - Each trade ends at its OWN stop or target. One trade hitting its stop
    or target does not touch the others.
  - An OPPOSITE CHOCH closes EVERY open trade at the next candle's open -
    in all three modes, BOS only included - when "close and reverse" is
    ON (the default). If that CHOCH is a signal you trade ("CHOCH only"
    or "CHOCH and BOS"), the reverse setup is armed on the SAME candle,
    its order is placed on the next candle once the old trades are
    closed, and it becomes a trade when price comes back to its entry -
    like every setup. In the simulator 91% of these opposite CHOCHs
    armed the reverse setup and 61% of those filled. In "BOS only" the
    CHOCH closes the trades but opens nothing: the next BOS the other
    way does.
  - An opposite BOS you trade also closes everything (it can only come
    after an opposite CHOCH anyway).
  - The 02:00 force-close, the weekend cutoff and a news window (set to
    "Close any open trade") close every trade.

THE WAITING ORDER. Only ONE order waits at a time, as before. A new
signal replaces an unfilled one. While trades are open, the waiting order
is always the same way as them - the strategy never places an order
against its own open trades.

EXAMPLE, "CHOCH and BOS", long, R 3:
  CHOCH up. Rule 1 buys 4310, stop 4302, target 4334. Filled.
  BOS up at 4325. The higher timeframe agrees: rule 2 buys the broken
    pivot 4325, stop at the pullback low 4316, target 4352. Filled - two
    trades open.
  Price reaches 4334: trade 1 hits its target and closes. Trade 2 stays.
  BOS up at 4345: a third setup is armed, and so on.
  CHOCH down at 4331: trade 2 (and any other still open) is closed at the
    next open, and a short setup is armed.

PROPERTIES -> PYRAMIDING = 100. It is the most trades TradingView lets
be open at once; v7.1 sets it to 100. If your chart still shows 0 or 1,
type 100 - otherwise every extra BOS trade is refused without a word.
You may type a smaller number as a hard limit, e.g. 3.

RISK - READ THIS. Every trade risks your FULL base risk. With 4 trades
open, 4 x 50 = 200 is at risk at the same moment, and one sharp reversal
can stop them all - their stops are at different levels, but close
together. On your gold settings the simulator had up to 7 trades open
at once. What protects you:
  - "Max leverage" (group 21) now covers ALL open trades together. A new
    trade only gets the room that is left, and is skipped (size zero)
    when there is none.
  - "Max trades per day" counts every trade, BOS trades included.
  - "Stop for the day after losing this much" counts the total.
  - Loss recovery (Rule A / B) raises the risk of EVERY new trade after
    losses - with several trades at once that grows very fast. Keep it
    Off, or keep the hard cap low.

AUTOMATION - one TheConnector tag per trade. Every trade carries its own
tag: the tag text plus the trade's own 4-character code, made from the
signal candle's time (v7.2), for example smcgold-k3f9 and smcgold-k3g1.
The robot stamps it on the position and closes only the position
carrying it, so one trade hitting its stop never closes another.
When the strategy closes everything (opposite CHOCH, 02:00, weekend,
news), it closes trade by trade, each with its own message. So:
  - Keep "TheConnector tag" short: 7 letters or less (smcgold is fine).
    With the "-" and the 4-character code it must fit TheConnector's 12.
  - Never leave the tag blank when trades can stack.
  - Switch versions while flat (see the top of these notes).
  - PineConnector format: its closelong / closeshort close EVERY long /
    short on the symbol - with stacked trades use TheConnector. The JSON
    format now carries "trade":"Lk3f9" so a custom bridge can tell
    trades apart.

THE TABLE. "Trades entered" shows how many are still open right now,
for example "312  (3 still open)" - see "COUNTING TRADES". The audit
labels mark every BOS that added a trade as R1 / R2 ARMED, then FILLED
or CANCELLED as usual.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
COUNTING TRADES  -  the table and TradingView               (NEW in v7.2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Trades entered  312  (3 still open)" means: since the first candle on
the chart the strategy has entered 312 trades in total. 309 of them have
ended (stop, target or closed) and 3 are still running right now.
312 = 309 ended + 3 still open.

The row under it is TradingView's own count: "309 closed + 3 open". Both
add up to the same number. If they ever do not, the row turns orange
and the "Margin calls (Properties)" row shows why - TradingView's
margin-call simulation cuts a trade into pieces (see "MARGIN CALLS").

Four things are easy to mix up when you compare numbers:
  - "Total trades" in the Strategy Tester counts only trades that have
    ENDED. A trade still open at the right edge of the chart is not in
    it: 309 there, 312 in the table.
  - The List of Trades shows every trade on TWO lines, its entry and
    its exit, under one number. Count the numbers (#), not the lines.
  - The chart marks every trade TWICE: one arrow where it entered
    (L... / S...) and one where it ended (... exit, or the reason).
  - "CHOCH signals on the chart" and "BOS signals on the chart" count
    signals, not trades. Many signals never become a trade: outside
    hours, the minimum stop, a new signal first, price never came back
    to the entry. The signal audit labels show why for each one.

Up to v7.1, with stacked trades, TradingView's default "first in, first
out" rule could also cut one trade into two pieces in the List of
Trades, so TradingView showed more trades than the table. Fixed in v7.2.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TIME-OF-DAY FILTERS  -  Auto / Always apply / Never apply   (NEW in v2.3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the fix for the bug you found: the daily chart took zero trades.

Here is what was wrong. The session window checked the bar's own timestamp.
On a 1-minute chart every bar has a different time of day, so the window
does its job. On a DAILY chart every bar has only ONE time of day - the
moment the session opens. So the window stopped being a filter and became
an on/off switch for the entire chart. For OANDA gold the daily bar is
stamped at the 17:00 New York rollover, which is about 02:30-03:30 in
India - outside your 06:00-23:00 window and inside your 02:00 force-close
zone. Every single bar was rejected. 190 years of chart data, zero trades.

The new setting controls the session window, the force-close hour and the
weekend cutoff together:

  Auto          Default. They apply on intraday charts and switch OFF on
                1D, 1W and 1M, where they cannot mean anything sensible.

  Always apply  Force them on everywhere. On 1D and above the session
                window still cannot reject a bar (a daily bar spans every
                hour of the day, so it always overlaps your window), but
                the force-close and weekend rules do run.

  Never apply   No time rules at all, on any chart. The strategy trades
                whenever a signal appears.

Second part of the same fix: on intraday charts the window is now judged by
whether the bar OVERLAPS your hours, instead of only where the bar opens.
A strategy decides at the bar's CLOSE, so judging it by the bar's open is
wrong by one bar-length. On 1m that is one minute and changes nothing. On
4h it was throwing away real signals.

Measured over a week of bars, 06:00-23:00 India time, gold:

    chart     v2.2 bars allowed     v2.3 bars allowed
    1m              70.8%                 70.8%     unchanged
    5m              70.8%                 70.8%     unchanged
    15m             70.8%                 70.8%     unchanged
    30m             70.8%                 70.8%     unchanged
    1h              70.8%                 75.0%     recovered
    4h              66.7%                 83.3%     recovered
    12h             50.0%                100.0%     recovered
    1D               0.0%                100.0%     was dead
    1W               0.0%                100.0%     was dead
    1M               0.0%                100.0%     was dead

Your 1-minute setup is bit for bit unchanged. Nothing you have already
tested moves.

ON TIME, AT EVERY TIMEFRAME (v4.0). Every time rule is now judged at the
moment the strategy ACTS - the close of a bar - and it asks one question:
"will any part of the NEXT bar fall inside a no-trade period?" If yes, it
flattens and stops now. So it is exactly on time whenever a bar closes on
the boundary, and when the boundary falls in the middle of a bar it acts at
the last bar close BEFORE it - early, never late.

Tested with a position held open permanently, every timeframe, first
action relative to the boundary (+ = late, - = early):

    chart    02:00 force-close     Fri 21:00 weekend     17:25 news window
             v3.3       v4.0       v3.3       v4.0       v3.3        v4.0
    1m       +1 min     0          +1 min     0          +1 min      0
    5m       +5         0          +5         0          +5          0
    15m      +15        0          +15        0          +20         -10

On 1-minute and 5-minute charts every rule is now to the minute.

A warning that is not a bug: the daily chart will now trade, but a 1:1 stop
and target on daily gold bars is a completely different instrument from
your 1-minute setup. The stops will be hundreds of dollars wide and the
money-based sizing will hand you very small positions. It works correctly.
That does not mean it is good. Backtest it on its own terms before you put

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SESSION WINDOW, FORCE-CLOSE HOUR, TIMEZONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Session timezone            Default Asia/Kolkata. Every hour setting in
                              the strategy is read in this timezone.
  Take entries from (hour)    Default 6.
  Stop taking entries at      Default 23.
  Force-close at (hour)       Default 2. Any open trade is closed at market.

Outside the window the strategy pulls any resting order and arms nothing
new. It does NOT close a trade that is already running - that is what the
force-close hour is for.

The force-close works on a DEAD ZONE, not on a single hour. The dead zone
is everything from the force-close hour to the start of the session. The
first bar that lands inside it triggers the close. This matters because a
broker gap or a weekend can mean no bar exists at exactly 02:00 - with a
single-hour test the close would simply never happen and the trade would
run on. With a dead zone it fires on the first bar after the market
reopens, whenever that is.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
STOP LOSS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The stop goes at the opposite live structure level - the CHOCH* level on
the other side of the trade - plus a buffer you set in price. While a
rule-1 setup waits in Continuous mode, its stop follows that live level;
a rule-2 setup keeps the stop it was armed with (v7.0 - see "RULE 2 KEEPS
ITS BRACKET").

For a long, that is the live swing low level, minus the buffer.
For a short, the live swing high level, plus the buffer.

If that level does not exist yet, no trade is armed. The stop is not
invented.

A sanity check runs before every order: the entry must be on the correct
side of the stop. If the market has moved so far that it is not, the setup
is cancelled outright rather than left resting with a stop on the wrong
side. (That was a real bug in an earlier version - the check existed but
the already-resting order was left alive.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TARGET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
An R multiple of the entry-to-stop distance. Default 1.0, so a 1:1. Set 2.0
for a 1:2 and so on.

With "push the target out to cover the commission" on (the default), the R
multiple is measured on NET money, after the broker's charge, not on the
raw price distance. A 1:1 means a win pays exactly what a loss costs.

Both the stop and the target are attached to the position as a bracket the
moment it opens, so they are live at the broker even if the chart is shut.

FROZEN AT FILL (v4.0). Once a trade is open, its stop and target never move
- inside TradingView or at your broker. They are exactly the values that
were sent to FxPro with the entry. v3.x kept recalculating TradingView's
copy of the target from the actual fill price, so after a gapped fill the
two could disagree about where the trade should end.

COMMISSION MODEL  -  what your broker charges              (NEW in v3.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Your broker charges 28 USD per lot to open a gold trade and 28 to close it.
56 for the complete round turn. Until v3.0 the strategy did not know that,
so when you asked it to risk 50 it sized the position off the price move
only - and the trade actually cost you more than 50.

How much more depends entirely on how tight the stop is:

    stop      old qty     commission     what the "50 risk" REALLY cost
     3 pts    16.6667 oz      9.33            59.33      18.7% over
     5 pts    10.0000 oz      5.60            55.60      11.2% over
    10 pts     5.0000 oz      2.80            52.80       5.6% over
    25 pts     2.0000 oz      1.12            51.12       2.2% over
    50 pts     1.0000 oz      0.56            50.56       1.1% over

On your 1-minute gold setup, where the stops are tight, nearly one fifth of
your risk was going somewhere you had not counted it.

Five models, pick the one that matches your broker:

  Off                     Exactly like v2.3. Commission ignored in sizing.

  Auto - per lot,         DEFAULT. You give it the charge per LOT for ONE
  contract size from      side (0 until you type yours - v6.1; it was
                          28 before). It works out how many units
  the ticker              are in a lot from the ticker itself. It doubles
                          the charge for the round turn.

  Per lot - I set the     Same, but you type the units per lot yourself
  contract size           when the auto guess is wrong for your broker.

  Percent of trade value  For crypto and shares, where the fee is a
                          percentage of the notional.

  Fixed cash per order    A flat fee per order whatever the size.

Enter every figure in your ACCOUNT currency.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW THE CONTRACT SIZE IS DETECTED FROM THE TICKER      (NEW in v3.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the part that makes one setting work across every instrument. A
"lot" is a different number of units on every market, so the strategy reads
the ticker and works it out:

    crypto (checked first, v6.0)       ->       1 unit per lot
    ticker contains XAU, or GOLD       ->     100 units per lot
    ticker contains XAG, or SILVER     ->    5000 units per lot
    ticker contains XPT or XPD         ->     100 units per lot
    a forex pair                       ->  100000 units per lot
    anything else (indices)            ->       1 unit per lot

These match every figure you checked at FxPro: forex 100,000, gold 100
oz, silver 5,000 oz, BTC and ETH 1 coin, US30 and US500 1 unit.

So your 28 per lot per side becomes:

    XAUUSD     28 / 100      =  0.28  per ounce per side  (0.56 round turn)
    XAGUSD     28 / 5000     =  0.0056 per ounce per side
    EURUSD     28 / 100000   =  0.00028 per unit per side

That 0.28 is exactly the figure you worked out yourself - 0.01 lot of gold
costing about 28 cents a side. The maths agrees with your broker statement.

IMPORTANT, and this is the one thing to watch. For an index, a crypto pair
or a share, "units per lot" is 1. At FxPro that is right: 1 lot of BTC is
1 coin and 1 lot of US30 is 1 unit. With 28 in the box, the strategy
charges 28 per coin or per index unit each side. If your broker charges
those markets less, or only through the spread, the strategy will size
your positions down to almost nothing. Check it with one 0.01-lot trade
(see "SETTING UP A NEW TICKER"). Two things protect you:

  - The table row "Commission model" prints the contract size it detected,
    so you can see it before you trade.
  - The table row "Costs last trade" prints the commission as a
    percentage of your risk, and turns RED above 25%.

If your broker charges an index or a coin differently, set the real
charge per lot on that chart, or use "Percent of trade value", "Fixed
cash per order" or "Off" there.

HOW COMMISSION CHANGES THE POSITION SIZE               (NEW in v3.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before: quantity = risk / stop distance.
Now:    quantity = risk / (stop distance + round-turn commission per unit).

That one change makes your risk figure mean exactly what it says. With 28
per lot per side on gold:

    stop     risk       quantity      lots      commission     loss if stopped
     3 pts     50      14.0449 oz    0.1404        7.87            50.0000
     5 pts     50       8.9928 oz    0.0899        5.04            50.0000
    10 pts     50       4.7348 oz    0.0473        2.65            50.0000
    50 pts     50       0.9889 oz    0.0099        0.55            50.0000
   300 pts     50       0.1664 oz    0.0017        0.09            50.0000

50 means 50. Every time, at every stop distance, commission included.

Checked across five commission models, four stop distances, four R

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PUSH THE TARGET OUT TO COVER THE COMMISSION            (NEW in v3.0, ON)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sizing alone fixes the losing side only. If the target stays where it was,
a winner pays LESS than a loser costs, because commission comes off the win
as well as adding to the loss. On gold with 50 of risk:

    stop  3 pts    loss 50.00    win 34.27     a winner pays 15.73 less
    stop 10 pts    loss 50.00    win 44.70     a winner pays  5.30 less
    stop 50 pts    loss 50.00    win 48.89     a winner pays  1.11 less

On a 1:1 system that asymmetry alone turns a 50% hit rate into a losing
account. Nothing else has to go wrong.

With this ON (the default) the target is moved far enough out that a win
nets your FULL R after commission. Loss exactly 50, win exactly 50, at
every stop distance. Set the R multiple to 2 and a win nets exactly 100.

THIS IS THE ONE SETTING IN v3.0 THAT MOVES YOUR EXISTING BACKTEST NUMBERS.
The target is now slightly further away, so slightly fewer targets are
reached. Switch it off and the targets go back to exactly where v2.3 put
them. It does nothing in "Fixed cash per order" mode, where the fee does
not scale with position size.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MAKING TRADINGVIEW'S OWN REPORT MATCH                  (NEW in v3.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
There is one thing the script cannot do for you. TradingView's commission
setting lives in the Properties tab of the strategy, and Pine cannot write
to it from code - it has to be a fixed number typed into the declaration,
which would then be wrong for every other ticker. So:

  The strategy uses your commission for SIZING and for its own counters
  automatically. That part needs nothing from you, and it is the part that
  decides how much real money is at risk on a live trade.

  TradingView's built-in performance report - net profit, the equity curve,
  the list of trades - only knows about commission if you type it into
  Properties.

To make the report exact, the table gives you the number:

  1. Read the "Properties cash/contract" row in the table. On XAUUSD with
     28 per lot per side it says 0.28.
  2. Settings -> Properties tab -> Commission.
  3. Set the type to "Cash per contract" and paste that number in.

TradingView then charges it on the entry order and again on the exit order,
which is the round turn. Do this once per chart.

No double counting - and this time that is actually true. TradingView's
per-trade profit already has the Properties commission taken off, so when
Properties has a commission set, the strategy uses that profit as it is.
Only when Properties is empty does it subtract its own estimate.

A correction: the v3.x notes promised exactly this, and the v3.x code did
not do it - it subtracted the commission a second time. That is bug 3 in
"WHAT THE v4.0 AUDIT FOUND".

With the spread model on (group 26), the "Paste into Properties
cash/contract" row includes half the spread as well - paste that number,
not the bare 0.28.

WHICH NUMBERS ARE BEFORE COSTS AND WHICH ARE AFTER          (NEW in v4.1)

You asked this directly, so here it is as a list. With commission set ONLY
in the strategy settings (group 23) and nothing in Properties:

  AFTER costs (the strategy's own numbers - always, automatically):
    - the position size and the target
    - Losses carried, Risk NEXT trade, the daily loss limit
    - Costs last trade, Costs paid total

  BEFORE costs (TradingView's report - until you fill in Properties):
    - Net Profit, the equity curve
    - List of Trades: the Net P&L and Cumulative P&L columns
    - Profit factor, average trade, drawdown

So a Cumulative P&L of 4,908 with Properties empty is the price movement
only. Take off the table's "Costs paid total" to get what you would have
kept: 4,908 - 1,031.52 = about 3,876. Easier: paste the table's
"Paste into Properties cash/contract" number into Properties and every
TradingView figure becomes after-costs by itself.

Setting it in BOTH places is correct and does NOT count it twice. When
Properties has a commission, the strategy sees that TradingView has
already taken it off and does not take it off again. (Bug 3 of v4.0 was
exactly this, and it is fixed.)

What Properties changes: only TradingView's report. Sizes, targets,
entries, exits and webhook messages are identical either way.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
BROKER SPREAD  -  the new setting in group 26                (NEW in v4.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TradingView fills your backtest at the chart price and never charges a
spread. FxPro charges it on every trade: you buy at the ask and sell at the
bid. On 1-minute gold with a 3 to 5 point stop, the spread alone can be 5
to 10 percent of your risk - and it is the largest single reason a live
account earns less than its backtest.

Type your typical spread in PRICE - on FxPro gold usually around 0.20 to
0.40. Read it off the spread column in MT5's Market Watch at the hours you
actually trade. The strategy then treats it exactly like commission:

  - the position is sized so a stop-out costs your risk amount INCLUDING
    the spread;
  - the target is pushed out so a win still nets your full R;
  - the strategy's own P&L counters include it;
  - the "Properties cash/contract" table row includes half of it per side,
    so pasting that into Properties makes TradingView's report charge it.

It changes NOTHING that is sent to your broker. 0 = not modelled.

Spreads differ by market and through the day, so type the one for the
ticker on each chart.

What it did in the simulation - same strategy, same market, backtest versus
a simulated live FxPro account with 0.30 spread and about 3 seconds of
bridge delay:

                                  backtest     live        gap
    spread not modelled           -12.34      -18.38      -6.05 per trade
    spread 0.30 modelled          -15.03      -14.43      +0.60 per trade

Without it the backtest overstated live by about 12% of your risk on every
single trade. With it the two agree to within 60 cents a trade, and the
win rates agree within about one percentage point. The backtest ends up
very slightly CONSERVATIVE, which is the safe direction.

Notice the live column improved too, from -18.38 to -14.43. Modelling the
spread does not only make the backtest honest - risk-based sizing stops
ignoring a real cost, so it stops over-sizing tight stops. (Both columns
are negative because the simulated market is a random walk with no edge
for anyone. Only the GAP carries over to real gold.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
POSITION SIZE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three modes:

  Risk-based off the stop   Default, and the one you want. You set the
                            money you are willing to lose. The strategy
                            works out what one unit loses at your stop,
                            converts it into your account currency, and
                            buys exactly enough units that the stop costs
                            that amount. 50 means 50 on gold, on EURUSD,
                            on NAS100 - you never touch a lot size.

  Fixed quantity            Always the same number of units.

  Fixed cash per trade      Always the same notional value.

The "Last trade risk" row in the table shows what the filled trade ACTUALLY
had at risk, commission included. It should match your base risk. If it does
not, something (usually the leverage cap) intervened, and the "Trades cut
by leverage cap" row will say so.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
ROUNDING THE SIZE TO YOUR BROKER'S LOT STEP        (NEW in v3.2, ON)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Brokers only accept lot sizes in fixed steps. Almost always 0.01 lots - two
decimal places. TradingView does not know that, so up to v3.1 the backtest
was trading 0.0828 lots while your broker would have taken 0.08. Every
number in the report was slightly wrong, and always in a direction you
could not see.

v3.2 rounds the size the STRATEGY trades to the same step your broker uses.
Normal rounding, exactly as you described it:

    0.0828  ->  0.08          below the halfway point, rounds down
    0.0849  ->  0.08
    0.0850  ->  0.09          halfway and above, rounds up
    0.0851  ->  0.09
    0.0899  ->  0.09
    0.0050  ->  0.01
    0.0049  ->  0.00          too small - the setup is skipped

The step is in LOTS and is converted using the contract size from the
commission group. So 0.01 lots is 1 ounce on gold, and 1000 units on a
forex pair. Set it to 1 for a market that trades in whole contracts or
shares. 0 turns it off and behaves exactly like v3.1.

THE CONSEQUENCE, AND IT IS THE RIGHT ONE. Your risk is no longer exactly
the amount you set. It lands a little above or a little below. On gold with
50 of risk:

    stop      ideal oz    traded    lots     real risk    off by
     3 pts     14.0449      14      0.14        49.84      -0.3%
     5 pts      8.9928       9      0.09        50.04      +0.1%
    10 pts      4.7348       5      0.05        52.80      +5.6%
    25 pts      1.9562       2      0.02        51.12      +2.2%
    50 pts      0.9889       1      0.01        50.56      +1.1%
   100 pts      0.4972       0      0.00        SKIPPED
   300 pts      0.1664       0      0.00        SKIPPED

This was ALWAYS happening on your live account. v3.2 just stops the
backtest pretending otherwise. The "Last trade risk" row now shows the real
figure and the lot size beside it.

Two things to take from that table. First, the error is worst when the
position is smallest, because one step is then a big slice of the whole
trade. Raising your risk makes it finer: at 200 of risk a 10-point stop
lands 0.3% out instead of 5.6%. Second, a wide stop with a small risk
becomes UNTRADEABLE - 50 of risk on a 100-point gold stop is half of the
smallest lot your broker will take, so the setup is skipped rather than
sent as an empty order. That is the honest answer, not a bug.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ROUNDING OVERSHOOT LIMIT                       (NEW in v3.2, 25%)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rounding up is harmless when the position is large and dangerous when it is
small, because at the bottom of the range one step is an enormous jump.

I found this in simulation while building v3.2, and it would have been a
real problem. On gold with 200 of risk and a 300-point stop, the ideal size
is 0.0066 lots. Rounded normally that becomes 0.01 - and 0.01 lots on a
300-point stop is 300 of real risk, not 200. Half as much again as you
asked for, silently, on a live account.

So: if rounding UP would push the real risk more than this far above the
amount you set, the size is rounded DOWN instead. If rounding down reaches
zero the setup is skipped, which is correct - that trade is too small for
your broker to take at your risk setting.

25% is the default and normal trades are nowhere near it. The worst case in
the table above is 5.6%. Only the extreme small-size cases are caught. Set
0 for no limit and plain rounding everywhere.

It only applies to risk-based sizing. Fixed quantity and fixed cash are
sizes you set yourself, so they are left alone.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MAX LEVERAGE  -  and the bug it fixed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This one is worth understanding because it cost you 131 trades.

You had a backtest where only 2 or 3 orders ever filled. The cause: with a
10,000 account, risk-based sizing on a tight stop asks for an enormous
position. A 10-point stop on gold at 50 risk is about 21,000 of position.
TradingView silently REJECTS any order the account cannot hold. No error,
no marker, nothing in the list of trades. The orders were being thrown away.

And it is backwards from what you would expect: the TIGHTER the stop, the
BIGGER the position risk-based sizing demands. So the best-looking setups
were exactly the ones being dropped.

Two things fix it. The strategy declares zero margin requirement, and this
setting caps the position at equity x leverage (default 30, normal for
retail gold and FX). Watch the "Trades cut by leverage cap" row - if it
is climbing, your risk per trade is too large for the account.

Since v3.2 the cap is applied AFTER rounding, and when it bites the size is
rounded DOWN to the lot step rather than sitting just above the cap. A cap
that gets rounded back over the limit is not a cap.

Since v7.1 the cap covers ALL open trades together: a new trade only gets
the room left under the cap, and is skipped when there is none.

Since v6.0 the position value is converted to your account currency
first. Before that, a pair quoted in yen (USDJPY, price about 150) had
its cap about 150 times too small. Gold and anything quoted in USD are
unchanged.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MARGIN CALLS  -  what they are and how to stop them        (NEW in v4.1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT A MARGIN CALL IS. When you trade with leverage, the broker lends you
most of the position. You only put up a deposit, called margin. At 30x
leverage the deposit is 1/30 of the position's value. If the trade loses
so much that your account can no longer cover that deposit, the broker
closes some or all of the trade by force. That forced close is a margin
call.

TradingView can pretend to be that broker. The switch is in:

    Settings -> Properties -> "Long leverage"  and  "Short leverage"

(Older TradingView versions called these "Margin for long / short
positions", in percent. 30x leverage = 3.33% margin. Same thing.)

WHY YOUR TRADES WERE EXITING ON "MARGIN CALL". Your Properties leverage
was 30x AND the strategy's own "Max leverage" setting (group 21) was 30.
That combination makes every big trade start at 100% of your margin:

    Account 10,000.  Gold at 4,000.
    Rule B asks for a big trade. The strategy's Max leverage 30 cuts it
    to the most the account may hold:
        10,000 x 30 = 300,000 of gold = 75 ounces = 0.75 lots
    TradingView's deposit at 30x:
        300,000 / 30 = 10,000  ->  your WHOLE account
    Price moves 1.00 against you:
        loss 75, account 9,925, deposit still about 10,000
        -> not enough -> TradingView force-closes part of the trade.
           That part appears in the List of Trades as "Margin call".

Your stop was several points away. It never got a chance. TradingView
closes a slice - about four times what it needs, so it does not repeat
every bar - and the rest of the trade carries on. That is why you saw one
trade split into a 75.8 and a 0.2.

A real broker would not have done this. FxPro lets your account fall a
long way below the full deposit (to its "stop-out level") before it closes
anything, and a 75 loss on a 10,000 account is nowhere near that.
TradingView's version fires the moment the deposit is not fully covered.

HOW TO STOP IT - two settings, both needed:

  1. Properties -> Long leverage = 100x, Short leverage = 100x.
     The same 0.75-lot trade then needs a 3,000 deposit, so a margin call
     would need a 7,000 loss on ONE trade. With your stop and your risk
     cap that cannot happen, so margin calls simply stop.
     Rule of thumb: Properties leverage at least 3 times the strategy's
     Max leverage. Higher is fine.

  2. Inputs -> group 21 -> "Max leverage" = your REAL FxPro leverage for
     gold, or lower. This is the setting that actually limits your trade
     size. If it is higher than FxPro allows you, FxPro will refuse the
     big trades the backtest takes. (Check your account leverage in
     FxPro Direct or MT5.)

Then check: the table's "Margin calls (Properties)" row reads 0, and no
exit in the List of Trades says "Margin call".

The "Trades cut by leverage cap" row is still worth watching. With Loss
recovery B the trades grow fast - your table showed 554 of risk on the
next trade. If this row keeps climbing, the account is too small for the
ladder you have set, and the ladder cannot recover what it thinks it is
recovering.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
LOSS RECOVERY  -  Rule A and Rule B      (CHANGED in v2.3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Off by default. Read the warning at the end of this section before using it.

  Rule A - recover losses + base profit
        Next risk = (losses carried + base) / R multiple.
        One winner at your target gets everything back PLUS the base.
        At 1:1   50 -> 100 -> 200 -> 400 -> 800
        At 1:3   50 -> 33.33 -> 44.44 -> 59.26 -> 79.01
        (At 1:3 a win pays three times the risk, so a third is enough.
        After the first loss the risk is LOWER than the base.)

  Rule B - double the losses
        Next risk = 2 x losses carried, but never less than your base.
        50 -> 100 -> 300 -> 900 -> 2700 -> 8100
        That is when every loss is a full stop. It doubles the TOTAL
        carried, not the last trade. A trade closed early by an opposite
        signal loses less than its risk, so the next step is smaller.

  The "never less than your base" part is new in v4.0. Without it, a small
  leftover debt - 5, or 0.30, or a floating-point crumb - made Rule B ask
  for a position smaller than the smallest lot, the order rounded to zero,
  and because no trade could happen the debt could never be paid. Trading
  stopped for good. Debt under half a cent now counts as paid as well.

WHAT CHANGED IN v2.3, and it is the thing you asked for.

v2.2 asked one question when a trade closed: did it make money? Any profit
at all wiped the carried losses and dropped the next trade back to the base.

That is wrong, and your own backtest showed it. Trade 52 exited on an
opposite signal for +5.53 USD. That 5.53 erased several hundred dollars of
carried losses and the ladder restarted at 50 - even though not one cent of
those losses had actually been recovered.

v2.3 tracks the UNRECOVERED LOSS instead. Every closed trade adds its loss
or subtracts its profit. The ladder continues from whatever is still owed.

    three stops, then a win cut short by an opposite signal, Rule A:

      risk    50.00   stop     -50.00    owed      0 ->  50    next risk  100
      risk   100.00   stop    -100.00    owed     50 -> 150    next risk  200
      risk   200.00   stop    -200.00    owed    150 -> 350    next risk  400
      risk   400.00   partial  +44.00    owed    350 -> 306    next risk  356

      v2.2 would have made that last "next risk" 50.

A FULL target win still clears everything and resets to base - that happens
by arithmetic, not by a special case. Rule A is built so a win at your R
multiple returns losses + base, which is always more than the debt. Rule B
at 1:1 returns double the debt. Verified across 54,000 simulated sequences:
273,518 full wins, every one of them cleared the debt to exactly zero.

Two smaller fixes in the same area:
  - Commission is subtracted from every closed trade before it is counted.
    In v3.0 this works whether or not you set commission in Properties: the
    real figure is used when it is there, the strategy's own estimate when
    it is not, never both.
  - A halt caused by the risk cap now clears only when the debt is actually
    paid off, not on any small win.

THE WARNING. Both rules make the position bigger after a loss. That is the
fastest known way to empty an account. Rule B from a 50 base:

    50 -> 100 -> 300 -> 900 -> 2700 -> 8100 -> 24300 -> 72900

Seven losses in a row and it is asking for more than most accounts hold.
Seven losses in a row is not rare. The hard cap below exists for exactly
this reason - do not remove it. My honest advice: leave both rules off
until you have watched the base strategy trade for a long time.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
HARD CAP ON RISK PER TRADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The recovery ladder can never ask for more than this (default 500). When it
is reached, one of three things happens, your choice:

  Clamp to the cap and carry on   Take the cap as the risk and keep going.
                                  The sequence can no longer recover
                                  everything, but it keeps trading.

  Stop for the rest of the day    Default. Stop opening trades, clear the
                                  carried losses, start fresh tomorrow.
                                  This draws a line under a bad run
                                  instead of chasing it.

  Stop permanently                No more trades until you reload the
                                  strategy. Use it when you want the
                                  backtest to show you exactly where the
                                  sequence broke.

An earlier version had a real deadlock here: the halt was only cleared by a
winning trade, but the halt blocked all entries, so no trade could open to
produce that win. It is now cleared by the daily reset or by the debt being
paid, never by a condition the halt itself makes impossible.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPPOSITE SIGNAL WHILE IN A TRADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Pine strategy cannot be long and short at the same time - that is a
platform limit, not a choice. It can hold several trades the SAME way
(v7.1, a trade for every same-side BOS).

  ON (default)   EVERY running trade is closed at market and the new one
                 is opened. An opposite CHOCH closes them in all three
                 modes, BOS only included (v7.1).
  OFF            The new signal is ignored until the current trade finishes.

Only signals from sources you switched on count. In "CHOCH only" mode a BOS
will not reverse your trade.

v4.0: the CLOSE works even while a guard has trading halted - daily loss
limit, max trades, risk cap, drawdown, news window, weekend. A halt stops
NEW trades. It no longer stops you getting out of one the structure says
is wrong. The reversal into the new direction still waits for the halt to
lift.

A same-direction signal while you are in a trade never moves the target
of a running trade. A same-side BOS adds a trade of its own (v7.1, "BOS
only" and "CHOCH and BOS"); a same-side CHOCH is ignored.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
ENTRY ORDER HANDLING  -  Continuous / Send once
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Continuous - follow the level   Default. While a setup waits, the limit
                                  is re-placed every bar at the recomputed
                                  level, so a rule-1 entry ratchets with
                                  each new extreme of the leg. The stop
                                  follows the live CHOCH* level and the
                                  size is worked out again each time.

  Send once - place it and leave  The level is worked out once when the
                                  setup arms, and the order is left exactly
                                  there. Entry, stop, target and size are
                                  all frozen. No ratchet, no chasing. What
                                  you saw when the signal printed is what
                                  you get.

Neither one changes how many webhook messages you get. A TradingView
strategy alert fires when an order FILLS, not when it is placed. Re-placing
a resting limit sends nothing to your bridge, and FxPro never sees a
waiting order in either mode. Your broker hears about the trade once, when
TradingView fills it, and gets the entry, stop, target and size from that
moment.

ONE TRADE, BOTH WAYS. Your settings: rule 1 at 25%, R multiple 3, stop
buffer 1.0, risk 50, commission 28 per lot per side (0.56 per ounce round
turn), lot step 0.01. Gold on 1m.

  10:00  Bullish CHOCH. The leg runs from 4300.00 (the live CHOCH* low)
         up to 4312.00.
         Rule 1 level: 4312.00 - 25% of 12.00 = 4309.00
         Stop: 4300.00 - 1.00 buffer         = 4299.00   (10.00 away)
         Size: 50 / (10.00 + 0.56)           = 0.047 lots -> 0.05
         Target: 4309 + 3 x 10 + 2.24 (commission push-out) = 4341.24
         BOTH modes place: BUY LIMIT 0.05 at 4309.00, SL 4299.00,
         TP 4341.24. Nothing is sent to FxPro yet.

  10:01  Price makes a new high at 4316.00.

         CONTINUOUS  re-prices: level 4316 - 25% of 16 = 4312.00, stop
                     still 4299.00 (13.00 away), size 50 / 13.56 = 0.037
                     -> 0.04 lots, target 4312 + 39 + 2.24 = 4353.24.
         SEND ONCE   stays at 4309.00 / 4299.00 / 4341.24, 0.05 lots.

  10:04  Price dips to 4311.80 and turns up again.

         CONTINUOUS  FILLS at 4312.00. The alert fires and your broker
                     gets: buy 0.04 lots, SL 4299.00, TP 4353.24.
         SEND ONCE   does not fill - the dip stopped 2.80 above 4309.00.

  What happens next decides which mode did better on this trade:

    a) Price runs to 4354 without coming back.
         Continuous  hits its target: +162.72 (3 x its 54.24 risk).
         Send once   never fills. The order is pulled on the next signal
                     or by the expiry. 0.

    b) Price falls straight through 4309 to 4299.
         Continuous  stopped out: -54.24.
         Send once   fills at 4309, then stopped out: -52.80.

    c) Price dips to 4308.50, then rallies to 4342.
         Continuous  in the trade from 4312, target 4353.24 not yet
                     reached - still open.
         Send once   fills at 4309 and hits 4341.24: +158.40.

Both risk about 50 per trade (a little more after rounding to 0.01 lots),
both win exactly 3R when they win. The difference is WHICH trades you get:

  Continuous  more fills (it keeps chasing), entries closer to the top
              of the leg, a bit further from the stop. Catches the strong
              moves that never pull back far.
  Send once   fewer fills, only the deeper pullbacks, better prices.
              Misses the strong moves.

WHICH IS BETTER FOR AUTOMATION. For how closely live copies the backtest,
there is almost no difference - both reach the broker in the same way, one
market order per fill. Measured in the simulator (same market, 50 risk,
spread 0.30, about 3 seconds of bridge delay), gap between backtest and
live per trade:

                                  trades   gap per trade
    continuous, spread modelled    1242       +0.43
    send once,  spread modelled     915       +1.18
    continuous, spread NOT set     1242       -6.20
    send once,  spread NOT set      915       -4.95

So set the spread first - that matters ten times more than this choice.
Then choose the mode by your own backtest's net profit AFTER costs
(Properties commission set). If the two are close, take Send once: every
order is a level you can see on the chart and check against the broker,
nothing moves while you are not watching, and it is the only mode that
can later be upgraded to real pending orders at the broker (see "WHAT
COULD STILL BE BETTER").

(The simulated market has no edge for anyone, so only the GAP column
carries over to real gold - not whether either mode made money.)

WHEN EXACTLY DOES THE ENTRY FILL - AT THE CANDLE CLOSE OR ON THE TOUCH?

Two different moments:

  The LEVEL is worked out at each candle CLOSE. (Only in continuous mode
  does it change after the first time.)

  The FILL happens in REAL TIME, on the tick where price touches the
  level - in the middle of a candle, not at its close. The alert fires on
  that same tick, your bridge gets the message, and FxPro opens the trade
  at market, with the stop and target, a second or two later.

Your example, continuous mode, 25% pullback:

  Leg 4300 -> 4312. The order sits at 4309.00 (25% down from 4312).
  Price pulls back only 20%, to 4309.60, and turns up. No touch, no fill.
  (Say that small dip did not form a new structure low. If it did, the
  stop and the start of the leg move up with it - the level is always
  25% of the CURRENT leg.)
  Price climbs to 4320. At THAT candle's close the order moves to
      4320 - 25% of 20 = 4315.00   (stop 4299.00, size 0.03 lots,
                                    target 4365.24)
  A later candle dips to 4314.90. On the tick that touches 4315.00,
  TradingView fills, the alert goes out at once, FxPro buys 0.03 lots at
  market with SL 4299.00 and TP 4365.24.

  In send-once mode the order stays at 4309.00 the whole time, so the dip
  to 4314.90 does not fill it.

One detail: the level only moves at a candle close. If price makes a new
high and pulls back 25% from it inside the SAME one-minute candle, the
order is still at the old level during that candle.

All of this assumes Script execution = "On bar close" in the Properties
tab (see "THE PROPERTIES TAB").


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
CANCEL AN UNFILLED ORDER ON ANY NEW SIGNAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON by default. The moment a new signal prints, a resting order that has not
filled is pulled - even if the new signal does not itself produce a
tradeable setup.

Why this matters: before v2.2 the old order was only replaced when the new
signal actually armed something. If the new signal was rejected - no pivot
for rule 2, no opposite level, outside the session - the old order kept
sitting there against a market that had already moved on.

OFF returns to that older behaviour, where an order lives until it fills,
expires, or is replaced.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SAFETY GUARDS  -  every one optional, most off by default
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Cancel an unfilled order after N bars
      A setup that waited this long without filling is abandoned. Stops a
      stale level sitting in the market long after the move that made it.
      0 = never.

  Skip the setup if the stop is CLOSER than this
      A stop a couple of points away on 1m gold is inside the spread.
      Risk-based sizing reacts by buying an enormous position for it, and
      the spread alone can take you out. Try 3 on gold. 0 = off.

  Skip the setup if the stop is FURTHER than this
      The other end. A very wide stop makes the position tiny and the
      target unreachable in the time you have. 0 = off.

      Both of these are in PRICE - gold numbers. On another ticker, type
      that market's own values.

  Max trades per day
      Counted from filled entries, reset at the start of each trading day
      in your timezone. A hard brake on a choppy day. 0 = no limit.

  Stop for the day after losing this much
      Adds up closed profit and loss for the day. Once the day is down by
      this amount it stops opening trades and starts fresh tomorrow. The
      single most useful guard here - set it before you go live. 0 = off.

  Stop PERMANENTLY after an equity drawdown of this %
      Measured from the highest equity the account has reached. Unlike the
      daily stop this one does not reset. Last line of defence. 0 = off.

  Flatten and stop before the weekend
      ON by default, Friday 21:00. Closes any open trade and takes no new
      ones until the market reopens. Without it a Friday-night trade
      carries the weekend gap - and a gap does not respect your stop, it
      jumps straight past it. Cutoff day and hour are both settings.
      Follows the "Time-of-day filters" setting above, so on a daily chart
      under Auto it is off.

The table row "BLOCKED NOW" names whichever guard is currently stopping
trading, so you are never left guessing why nothing is firing.

v4.0: while any guard blocks entries, nothing stays armed. A setup that was
waiting is cancelled rather than left to be placed later from stale levels.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRADE 24/7  -  WEEKENDS                          (NEW in v3.3, OFF)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Group 24, below the weekend cutoff settings.

  OFF (default)  The weekend rules apply. The strategy flattens at the
                 cutoff and takes nothing until the market reopens. Right
                 for gold, forex, indices - anything that closes.

  ON             Saturday and Sunday are treated like any other day. For
                 crypto and anything else that runs round the clock, where
                 there is no weekend gap to protect against.

It overrides the weekend block completely, so you do not have to remember
to switch the cutoff off as well - switch this on and weekends are simply
allowed.

TWO THINGS TO BE CLEAR ABOUT.

First, this only unlocks SATURDAY AND SUNDAY. Your session hours in group
20 still apply, so with the default 06:00-23:00 you get weekend trading
during those hours only. For genuinely round-the-clock trading also set
"Time-of-day filters" in group 20 to "Never apply".

Second, DO NOT SWITCH THIS ON FOR GOLD. A position carried over the weekend
meets the Monday gap, and a gap does not respect your stop - it jumps
straight past it. The whole reason the weekend cutoff exists is that one
gap can cost more than a month of good trades. This toggle is for markets
that do not gap because they never close.

Checked in simulation over three full weeks: with it off, zero fills land
on a Saturday or Sunday. With it on, 706 of 2,423 fills do.

For crypto, switch it on on the crypto chart only. Each ticker has its
own chart and its own alert, so gold keeps its weekend cutoff.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
NEWS WINDOWS  -  MASTER SWITCH              (NEW in v7.0, the first setting)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
"News windows ON  (OFF = no news and no US-holiday blocks at all)" is the
very first setting in the panel, in its own group "0. NEWS WINDOWS -
master switch".

  ON (default)   Every news setting works exactly as you set it:
                   group 25  your own blackout windows
                   group 28  news windows only on chosen dates
                   group 29  automatic US news (NFP, jobless claims, ISM)
                             and the US-holiday block

  OFF            All of them are off in one go. No news window and no
                 US-holiday block, whatever those groups say. Trades are
                 taken freely inside your session hours (group 20, for
                 example 06:00 to 23:00).

What OFF does NOT touch: the session hours, the 02:00 force-close, the
weekend cutoff, the daily loss and trade limits, the risk cap, the

and they work again exactly as before - nothing to retype.

The table shows it: "News windows active on" reads "MASTER SWITCH OFF -
no news blocks", and the news / US-news rows read "master OFF".

As always, after switching it, delete the alert and create it again - an
alert keeps the settings it was created with.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
NEWS / BLACKOUT WINDOWS                          (NEW in v3.3, group 25)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three time windows in which the strategy will not trade. All off by
default. Use them on days when you know something is coming - a rate
decision, NFP, CPI - and you would rather be flat than in front of it.

  Window 1 / 2 / 3 - block trading      a tick box each
  - window N time                       a time range, e.g. 1725-1835
  When a window starts                  Close any open trade  /
                                        Leave open trades alone

MINUTES WORK. Click the time field and you get a proper picker. 17:25 to
18:35 means exactly that - not 17:00 to 19:00. Everything else in the
strategy that deals with time is hour-only; these windows are not.

Times are read in the SESSION TIMEZONE from group 20 - Asia/Kolkata by
default - not exchange time. A window may cross midnight: 2330-0030 works.

INSIDE A WINDOW:
  - no new setups are armed
  - any resting order is cancelled immediately
  - an open trade is closed, or left alone, depending on the setting
  - the table's "BLOCKED NOW" row reads "news window" so you know why
    nothing is happening

Windows can overlap. Three overlapping windows behave as one long one.

WHICH EXIT MODE TO USE. "Close any open trade" is the safe answer for a
real news release: a spike straight through your stop can fill far worse
than the price you set, and being flat beforehand is the only reliable
protection. "Leave open trades alone" keeps the running trade with its
stop and target and only blocks NEW entries - use it if you would rather
let a winner finish than be flattened by the clock.

TIMING (v4.0). The strategy flattens at the last bar close BEFORE the
window, so on a 1-minute or 5-minute chart you are flat AT 17:25, not a bar
after it. On bigger charts, where 17:25 falls inside a bar, it flattens at
the close before that bar - a little early, never late. v3.3 was one bar
late on every chart and on 4h never fired at all. You no longer need to
start windows a few minutes early.

Only the TIME of a window is read. If the time picker adds days of the
week, they are ignored - switch a window on for the news day and off
afterwards.

INTRADAY ONLY. On 1D, 1W and 1M charts the windows are ignored, because a
daily bar carries one single time of day - a blackout window there would
either block the entire chart or nothing at all. Same reason the session
filters switch off up there.

Checked in simulation: a single 17:25-18:35 window removed 135 of 1,775
fills, and across 96 configurations no order ever rested, no setup was ever
armed, and no position was ever left open without a close pending inside a
window.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
NEWS WINDOWS ON CHOSEN DATES                     (NEW in v5.0, group 28)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Off by default. It changes WHEN the news windows of group 25 apply, not
what they do.

  News windows work ONLY on the     ON / OFF.
  dates below                       OFF: the windows work every day, as
                                    before.
                                    ON: they only work on the dates listed.

  - dates (day of the month)        The days, separated by commas or
                                    spaces. Ranges work: 12-14.

  - these dates are for             This month only (default)
                                    Every month

How to type the dates. Any of these works:

    3, 12, 28          the 3rd, 12th and 28th
    12-14              the 12th, 13th and 14th
    3 12 17 28         spaces instead of commas
    12 - 14, 20        a range and a single day

Days 1 to 31 only. Anything else ("12th", "abc", "0") is ignored. The
table row "News windows active on" shows the dates the strategy actually
read - for example "3, 12, 28 of Oct 2026" - so you can see at once if it
understood you. If it is ON and nothing valid was typed, that row says NO
VALID DATES in red, and the windows block nothing at all.

EXAMPLE. US CPI on the 12th, NFP on the 3rd. You set:

    Group 25   Window 1 ON, 1750-1820       (18:00 IST release)
    Group 28   ON, dates "3, 12", This month only

    On the 3rd and the 12th: no trading 17:50 to 18:20, and an open trade
    is closed at 17:50 if "Close any open trade" is set.
    On every other day: the window does nothing. Trading as normal.

A window that crosses midnight belongs to the date it STARTS on. FOMC at
23:30 IST on the 17th, window 2325-0015: put 17 in the list. The window
still runs past midnight into the 18th - you do not need to add 18.

THIS MONTH ONLY versus EVERY MONTH:

  This month only   The dates apply to the current calendar month, the
                    month you are in right now. Earlier months in the
                    backtest are NOT blocked on those dates - you picked
                    the dates for this month's news, not for last month's.
                    When a new month starts, type the new dates and then
                    delete and re-create the alert. If you forget, the
                    windows simply stop working in the new month (the old
                    dates belong to the old month) - trading carries on as
                    if no window were set.

  Every month       The same day numbers apply in every month - the 12th
                    of January, of February, and so on. Useful in a
                    backtest when you want to see the effect of skipping
                    the same days everywhere.

Which calendar: the date is read in your session timezone (group 20,
Asia/Kolkata by default) - the same clock as the window times.

Remember: an alert keeps the settings it was created with. Every time you
change the dates, delete the alert and create it again.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
AUTOMATIC US NEWS SCHEDULE, US CLOCK AND US HOLIDAYS   (NEW in v5.1, 29)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A TradingView strategy cannot read an economic calendar or the internet.
So it cannot KNOW the news. What it can do is work out the dates of every
release that follows a fixed rule, from the date alone, forever. That is
what this group does.

THE RELEASES IT WORKS OUT

  NFP (jobs report)          08:30 New York
      The Bureau of Labor Statistics' own rule: the third Friday after
      the week (Sunday to Saturday) that contains the 12th of the
      previous month. Usually that is the first Friday of the month, but
      not always - in March 2024 it was the 8th. Two adjustments are
      built in: a January date on the 1st to 3rd moves a week later, and
      a date on a US holiday moves to the day before (July 4 on a Friday
      puts NFP on Thursday the 3rd).
      Checked against 30 real release dates - all of 2024, January to
      October 2025, and the awkward Januaries and July 4s back to 2015:
      30 of 30 correct.

  Weekly jobless claims      08:30 New York, every Thursday
      On a Thursday that is a US holiday (Thanksgiving, or Juneteenth /
      July 4 / Christmas / New Year falling on a Thursday) it comes out
      on the Wednesday before - and so does the window.

  ISM Manufacturing PMI      10:00 New York, 1st US working day
  ISM Services PMI           10:00 New York, 3rd US working day
      Working days skip weekends and US holidays. In January ISM
      publishes one working day later, and the rule follows that.
      Checked against every 2025 release and the Januaries of 2023 and
      2024.

Each has its own switch. They share two numbers: block from "minutes
BEFORE" (default 10) until "minutes AFTER" (default 20). Inside that
block the strategy behaves exactly as in a group 25 news window: no new
trades, any waiting order is cancelled, and an open trade is closed or
left alone according to "When a window starts" (group 25).

What it CANNOT work out: CPI, PPI, retail sales, FOMC and the rest - they
have no fixed rule. Put those in group 25 (times) and group 28 (dates)
as before. And a government shutdown delays everything; if that happens,
use group 28 for the real dates.

THE US CLOCK - SUMMER AND WINTER TIME

US clocks move forward on the second Sunday of March and back on the
first Sunday of November. India does not change. So a release at 08:30
New York is:

    US summer time (EDT, UTC-4)   18:00 in India
    US winter time (EST, UTC-5)   19:00 in India

  Auto (default)   Real New York time. The strategy moves by itself; you
                   never change anything twice a year.
  Always summer / Always winter
                   Pins the clock to one offset. Only for an override.

The table row "US clock" shows which is in use and what 08:30 New York
is in your session time right now, e.g. "summer EDT (auto)  08:30 NY =
18:00".

Example from the simulator, the week of 2 March 2026 (US clocks changed
on Sunday 8 March):

    Thu  5 Mar   jobless claims   blocked 18:50 to 19:20 India time
    Fri  6 Mar   NFP              blocked 18:50 to 19:20
    Thu 12 Mar   jobless claims   blocked 17:50 to 18:20   <- moved itself

READ THE GROUP 25 WINDOWS IN NEW YORK TIME (a tick box)

Off: your group 25 window times are in your session timezone, as before.
On: they are read on the US clock above, so you type them the way the US
calendar gives them - CPI "0825-0900", FOMC "1355-1430" - and they move
with US summer and winter time. The group 28 dates are still read on your
own calendar.

One limit, true of every window since v3.3: gold closes for about an hour
every day (around 17:00 to 18:00 New York). The strategy only acts when a
candle closes, so a window that STARTS while the market is shut is only
seen on the first candle after it reopens, and an open trade is closed at
the next open - one candle late. Do not start a window inside that hour.
No US release comes out then, so the automatic windows and the holidays
are never affected.

US MARKET HOLIDAYS

  New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Day (3rd Monday of January),
  Presidents' Day (3rd Monday of February), Good Friday (from Easter),
  Memorial Day (last Monday of May), Juneteenth (19 June, from 2022),
  Independence Day (4 July), Labor Day (1st Monday of September),
  Thanksgiving (4th Thursday of November), Christmas (25 December).

A fixed-date holiday on a Saturday is kept on the Friday before, on a
Sunday on the Monday after - except New Year on a Saturday, which US
markets do not move. Tested against the official lists for 2025, 2026
and 2027: all correct.

  Trade on US market holidays   ON (default): ordinary days, as before.
                                OFF: no trading from 00:00 to 24:00 New
                                York time on those days. In India that is
                                09:30 to 09:30 in summer, 10:30 to 10:30
                                in winter.

Gold still trades on most of these days, but thinly, with wide spreads
and an early close. The table row "US holidays" shows the next one, or
"TODAY" when it is one; BLOCKED NOW reads "US holiday".

SPEED

None of this slows the chart down. The dates for a day are worked out
once, when the New York day changes, and kept - not on every bar. The
look-ahead for the table rows runs on the last bar only. No extra data is
requested from TradingView.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROFIT TARGET  -  stop new trades                 (NEW in v4.2, group 27)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Off by default. When it is on, the strategy adds up the net result of
every CLOSED trade - after commission and spread, exactly the number in
TradingView's Cumulative P&L column once Properties commission is set -
and once that reaches your target it takes NO new trades.

  Stop taking new trades once the     ON / OFF.
  profit target is reached

  - profit target                     The amount, in USD. 200, 500, 1000.

  - count the profit                  Total since the start date
                                      Each day (starts again tomorrow)
                                      Each week (starts again on Monday)

  - start date (only for Total)       Trades that CLOSE on or after this
                                      moment are counted.

What it does NOT do:
  - It never closes a running trade. It is measured on closed trades, and
    this strategy holds one trade at a time, so when the target is
    reached you are already flat.
  - It does not reset the loss-recovery ladder. Rule A / B carry on from
    whatever is owed when trading starts again.
  - It sends nothing to your broker. It simply stops placing orders. A
    setup that was waiting is pulled inside TradingView, where nothing
    was at the broker anyway.

EXAMPLE 1  -  TOTAL, target 500:

    trade 1   +150    made 150
    trade 2    -50    made 100
    trade 3   +200    made 300
    trade 4   +250    made 550   -> REACHED. No more trades. Ever.

  It stays stopped. To trade again: raise the target (say to 1000) or move
  the start date forward - then delete and re-create the alert.

EXAMPLE 2  -  EACH DAY, target 200:

    Monday     +120, -40, +150   made 230  -> reached, stop for Monday
    Tuesday    starts again from 0
               -50, -50          made -100 -> keeps trading
               +250              made 150  -> keeps trading (under 200)

  A losing day never "stops" through this setting - use "Stop for the day
  after losing this much" (group 24) for that. The two work together: a
  profit ceiling and a loss floor for every day.

EXAMPLE 3  -  EACH WEEK, target 1000: the same as Example 2, but the count
starts from zero every Monday at midnight in your session timezone.

THE START DATE - READ THIS BEFORE GOING LIVE.
TradingView does not start a strategy at the moment you create the alert.
It first runs it over the whole chart history - the backtest - and then
carries on live. With "Total" and the default start date (2000), those
backtest trades count. If the backtest already made more than 500, your
live alert would start out stopped and never take a trade.

So for live trading with "Total": set the start date to the day you switch
the alert on. For a backtest: leave it at 2000 to count everything.
"Each day" and "Each week" do not need it - they count from the start of
the current day or week.

Where to see it: the table row "Profit target (made / target)" shows, for
example, "312.5 / 500 total". It turns green and adds "REACHED" when the
target is hit, and "BLOCKED NOW" then reads "profit target".

One honest limit: the count comes from TradingView's trades, not from
your broker's fills. If FxPro filled a little worse than TradingView, your
real account will be a little behind the table when the strategy stops.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MARKET TYPE  -  how it is worked out                          (NEW in v6.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The strategy reads the ticker and decides which kind of market it is. It
uses that for one thing only: how many units make one lot. It does NOT
set any commission or spread - you type both for each ticker.

It checks these in order and stops at the first one that matches:

  TradingView calls the symbol crypto          -> crypto, 1 per lot
  the ticker contains XAU or GOLD              -> gold, 100 per lot
  the ticker contains XAG or SILVER            -> silver, 5,000 per lot
  the ticker contains XPT or XPD               -> other, 100 per lot
  the base currency is a big coin (BTC, ETH,   -> crypto, 1 per lot
    SOL, XRP, LTC, ...)
  TradingView calls the symbol forex           -> forex, 100,000 per lot
  anything else                                -> index / other, 1 per lot

The table row "Ticker - type / 1 lot / symbol sent" shows what was
detected, for example "Gold  |  1 lot = 100  |  sends XAUUSD".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SETTING UP A NEW TICKER  -  the checklist                    (NEW in v6.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
YOUR FXPRO CONTRACT SIZES, and what the strategy detects for each. Both
match on every row. The lot step (0.01) and the minimum lot (0.01) are in
LOTS, so they fit every market as they are. Leave them.

  Market                    1 lot        0.01 lot
  XAUUSD gold               100 oz       1 oz
  XAGUSD silver             5,000 oz     50 oz
  EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD    100,000      1,000 units
  BTCUSD, ETHUSD            1 coin       0.01 coin
  US30, US500               1 unit       0.01 unit

COMMISSION AND SPREAD ARE YOURS TO TYPE, for each ticker. The strategy
turns them into a cost per unit with the lot size above. For a charge
of C per lot per side:

  Gold      2 x C / 100 per ounce          (C = 28 -> 0.56)
  Silver    2 x C / 5,000 per ounce        (C = 28 -> 0.0112)
  Forex     2 x C / 100,000 per unit       (C = 28 -> 0.00056 = 5.6 pips)
  Crypto    2 x C per coin                 (C = 28 -> 56)
  Indices   2 x C per index unit           (C = 28 -> 56 points)

plus the spread you type in group 26. The table row "Paste into
Properties cash/contract" then gives the exact number for TradingView's
Properties. Pairs quoted in another currency, such as USDJPY, are
converted for you.

HOW TO FIND YOUR NUMBERS FOR A TICKER:
  - Commission: open 0.01 lot and close it, then read the statement.
    The charge per lot per side is 100 x the charge on one side of that
    0.01-lot trade. (0.28 on the statement -> 28 per lot.)
  - Spread: the spread column in MT5's Market Watch, at the hours you
    trade. Use the top of the range you see.

ONE CHART AND ONE ALERT PER TICKER. An alert keeps the settings that
were in place when it was created. Set up each chart, then create that
chart's alert.

  1. Open the ticker on its own chart, 1 minute, and add the strategy.
  2. Table row "Ticker - type / 1 lot / symbol sent": check the market
     type, the lot size and the name sent.
  3. Commission and spread: type this ticker's charge per lot (group
     23) and spread (group 26). The "Commission model" row turns from
     orange to normal once the charge is typed.
  4. Distances: type price values for THIS market into group 20 (stop
     buffer) and group 24 (closer / further limits). A gold 3.0 minimum
     stop is 30,000 pips on EURUSD.
  5. Max leverage (group 21): at or below what FxPro gives you on this
     market. Crypto and indices often get far less leverage than gold.
  6. Weekends: for crypto, switch on "Trade 24/7" (group 24) on the
     crypto chart if you want Saturdays and Sundays.
  7. Symbol: type the broker's name in group 30 if it differs.
  8. Properties -> Commission -> Cash per contract: paste the table's
     "Paste into Properties" number for this ticker.
  9. Run the backtest. Look at "Costs last trade" (red above 25%) and
     "Skipped". If most setups are skipped, the costs are too big for
     1-minute stops on this market.
 10. Create the alert on this chart: condition "Order fills only",
     message ONLY {{strategy.order.alert_message}}, webhook URL
     https://webhook.theconnector.fr/YOUR_ACCESS_KEY.
 11. On the first live trade, check the lot size in MT5 yourself.

The TheConnector tag can stay the same on every chart, because each
close command also carries the symbol.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PROPERTIES TAB  -  every setting, exactly                (NEW in v4.1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Settings (gear icon on the strategy) -> Properties. These are the values
that make TradingView do exactly what your Inputs say - nothing added,
nothing taken away.

  Initial capital          Your real FxPro balance, in USD. The leverage
                           cap and the drawdown guard are worked out from
                           it. (The position size is not - that comes from
                           your risk amount.)

  Default order size       Leave it (1, Quantity). The strategy always
                           sends its own size; this box is never used.

  Pyramiding               100 (since v7.1). It is the most trades that
                           can be open at once. At 0 or 1 TradingView
                           refuses every extra BOS trade without a word.
                           Below 100 only if you want a hard limit, e.g.
                           3 = at most 3 trades open.

  Bar detalization         Leave "Default". If your plan offers a lower
                           timeframe here (Premium plans), you may pick
                           it: the backtest then knows whether the stop or
                           the target came first inside a candle. It does
                           not change live trading.

  Script execution         "On bar close" ONLY. Untick "On realtime bar
                           tick" and "After order is filled".
                           THIS ONE MATTERS MOST. With "On realtime bar
                           tick" ticked, the strategy recalculates on every
                           price tick of the live candle - so live, it
                           acts on CHOCH / BOS signals and levels from a
                           candle that has not finished, which can vanish
                           by the close. Your backtest never sees that; it
                           only ever sees finished candles. Live and
                           backtest stop matching.

  Commission               Type "Cash per contract". Value: the table's
                           "Paste into Properties cash/contract" row -
                           0.28 for your 28-per-lot gold commission, or
                           more if the spread setting (group 26) is on.
                           Only TradingView's report uses this. The
                           strategy never counts it twice.

  Long leverage            100x. See "MARGIN CALLS" for why - 30x was
  Short leverage           causing your margin-call exits.

  Slippage                 0. Your 200 ticks makes every stop and every
                           market close 0.20 worse in the backtest (gold's
                           tick is 0.001), and the strategy's sizing
                           cannot see it - so a stopped trade loses more
                           than the risk you set. Put your real trading
                           costs into group 26 "Broker spread" instead:
                           then the size, the target and the report all
                           include them.

  Limit order execution    "Requested price". The entry fills the moment
                           price touches your level - which is exactly
                           what the strategy, and the alert, assume.

  Order execution delay    "One tick". An order is filled on the next
                           price tick after it is placed - the normal
                           behaviour the strategy was built and tested on.

Then click OK.

AFTER CHANGING ANY SETTING, MAKE THE ALERT AGAIN. A TradingView alert
keeps a copy of the settings from the moment it was created. Changing
Inputs or Properties later does NOT change a running alert. Delete the old
alert and create it again (see "SETTING THE ALERT UP"), or the broker
keeps trading the old settings.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
GETTING LIVE TO MATCH THE BACKTEST  -  what to actually do
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The strategy's DECISIONS are now identical live and in the backtest: same
signals, same rule choice, same timing, same bracket. What can still differ
is the PRICE you get, and that is down to three things.

  1. Spread - the biggest one. Set group 26. Measured above: it closes the
     gap from about 6 per trade to under 1.

  2. Your broker enters at MARKET after TradingView confirms the fill.
     The strategy uses limit orders; the message goes out when TradingView
     sees the limit touched, and FxPro then buys at market a second or two
     later. On average that is a little worse than the limit price. This
     is the normal cost of alert-driven automation and no setting removes
     it. (A bridge that places real pending limit orders at the broker
     would - see "WHAT COULD STILL BE BETTER" at the end.)

  3. Different price feeds. Your chart is OANDA; your broker is FxPro.
     Structure levels are worked out from OANDA prices and traded at FxPro
     prices. FxPro became a TradingView partner broker in 2025 - if your
     account type lets you connect it, chart FxPro's OWN gold feed so the
     levels are your broker's levels. (Their TradingView link appears to be
     for cTrader-type accounts - check with FxPro for MT5.)

The checklist, in order:

  [ ] Properties tab set exactly as in "THE PROPERTIES TAB": Script
      execution "On bar close" only, leverage 100x, slippage 0. The
      table's "Margin calls (Properties)" row must read 0.
  [ ] Group 26: set "Broker spread" to your real gold spread (e.g. 0.30).
  [ ] Properties -> Commission -> Cash per contract -> paste the number
      from the table's "Properties cash/contract" row.
  [ ] Group 24: "Skip if the stop is CLOSER than" at least 3 to 5 times
      your spread. Tight stops are where friction eats the most.
  [ ] Continuous or Send once: compare both backtests after costs - see
      "ENTRY ORDER HANDLING" for a worked example of each.
  [ ] Group 22: "Minimum lot your broker accepts" = 0.01.
  [ ] Demo for two weeks. Compare trade by trade against the List of
      Trades. After 30 to 50 trades you have your real gap as a number.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
AUTOMATION  -  the short version
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE alert. Not four. You do not set up buy, sell, buy-close and sell-close
separately.

That is the whole reason this is a strategy and not an indicator. With an
indicator you would need one alert per condition. With a strategy,
TradingView fires the SAME alert every time an order fills and swaps in the
message belonging to that particular order. The same alert says buy on a
long entry, sell on a short entry and close on an exit. The strategy
decides which - you never do.

The chain:

    TradingView cloud -> an order fills -> the alert fires -> webhook URL
                                                                  |
                                                            bridge service
                                                                  |
                                                          your broker / MT5

Your computer can be off, because TradingView runs the alert on its own
servers. Whatever sits in the bridge box has to run 24/7, which normally
means a cheap VPS.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SETTING THE ALERT UP  -  the exact clicks
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Group 22: switch on "Attach a webhook message to every order".
  2. Pick the format your bridge expects, and set "Quantity to send".
  3. On the chart: Alerts -> Create Alert.
  4. Condition = SMC Structure Strategy, and choose ORDER FILLS.
  5. Message box:  {{strategy.order.alert_message}}
  6. Notifications tab -> tick Webhook URL -> paste your bridge's URL.
  7. Save. That one alert covers every trade from then on.
  8. Changed any Input or Properties setting later? Delete the alert and
     make it again. An alert keeps the settings it was created with.

Step 4 matters. Earlier notes said "Any alert() function call" - that is the
wording for an INDICATOR and it is wrong here. The strategy attaches its
message to each order, and those arrive through Order fills.

v4.0: "Order fills and alert() function calls" is now safe too. Up to v3.3
the strategy still fired the indicator's own BOS/CHOCH text alerts, and
that option would have sent them to your bridge. They are switched off.

Webhooks need a paid TradingView plan.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUANTITY TO SEND  -  lots or units       (NEW in v3.1, read this one)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most dangerous setting in the file. Wrong one way and every
trade is a hundred times too big.

Pine sizes in UNITS. On gold a unit is one ounce, so a trade might be 8.28.
MetaTrader bridges - PineConnector, TradingConnector, most MT4/MT5 tools -
expect LOTS. 8.28 LOTS of gold is 828 ounces. That is a 100x position, and
v3.0 sent exactly that.

  Lots (default)   Divides by the contract size from the commission group.
                   8.28 ounces goes out as 0.0828. This is what an MT4 or
                   MT5 bridge wants.

  Raw units        Sends 8.28. Correct for broker REST APIs that think in
                   units, such as OANDA.

If you are unsure, leave it on Lots. Sending lots to a units broker makes
the trade 100x too SMALL, which costs you nothing. The other way round
empties the account.

CHECK YOUR FIRST LIVE TRADE against your broker terminal before you walk
away from it. Look at the position size with your own eyes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MINIMUM LOT YOUR BROKER ACCEPTS          (NEW in v3.1, default off)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Brokers refuse an order below their minimum lot, usually 0.01. TradingView
does not know that, so the backtest happily fills a 0.0099-lot trade your
broker would throw away, and you are left wondering why a signal you watched
print never reached the account.

Set this to your broker's minimum and the strategy skips such a setup
instead of sending an order that gets rejected. Skipped ones are counted in
the "Skipped - stop / min lot / costs" row.

It bites when the stop is wide and the risk is small. 50 of risk on a
300-point gold stop is 0.0017 lots - untradeable at most brokers. Either
raise the risk or accept that very wide stops are not tradeable at your size.

0 = no check, exactly like v3.0. This is the same class of problem as the
leverage bug that silently swallowed 131 of your orders, which is why it is
here.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MESSAGE FORMATS  -  what actually gets sent
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four formats. The three below are what v3.1 produces, on XAUUSD, 8.28
ounces, licence 12345, in Lots mode. The fourth, TheConnector, has its own
section right after this one.

JSON - for your own bridge or a broker API:

  entry long   {"id":"12345","action":"buy","side":"long","symbol":"XAUUSD",
                "qty":0.0828,"entry":4300.000,"sl":4295.000,"tp":4305.600}
  exit         {"id":"12345","action":"close","side":"long","symbol":"XAUUSD",
                "qty":0.0828,"entry":4300.000,"sl":null,"tp":null}

PineConnector - for MT4 and MT5:

  entry long   12345,buy,XAUUSD,sl=4295.000,tp=4305.600,volume=0.0828
  exit long    12345,closelong,XAUUSD
  exit short   12345,closeshort,XAUUSD

Plain text - for a bridge that parses words:

  entry long   buy XAUUSD side=long qty=0.0828 entry=4300.000 sl=4295.000
               tp=4305.600
  exit         close XAUUSD side=long qty=0.0828 entry=4300.000

Two details worth knowing. The stop and target ride along with the ENTRY
message, so your broker holds the real bracket - it survives the bridge
crashing. And sl= / tp= are PRICES, not pips; check that against your
bridge's documentation, because some expect pips.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THECONNECTOR  -  setting it up, step by step              (NEW in v5.2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What the strategy sends (access key in the webhook URL, as TheConnector
recommends, so it is not in the message):

  entry long   {"action":"buy","symbol":"XAUUSD","volume":0.05,
                "sl_price":4299.000,"tp_price":4341.880,"tag":"smcgold-k3f9"}
  entry short  {"action":"sell", ... the same fields ...}
  exit long    {"action":"closelong","symbol":"XAUUSD","tag":"smcgold-k3f9"}
  exit short   {"action":"closeshort","symbol":"XAUUSD","tag":"smcgold-k3f9"}

  volume     the exact lot the strategy sized - your risk including
             costs, rounded to 0.01 - so the broker trades what the
             backtest did. TheConnector: "exact lots are sent with
             volume". Its own risk setting is not used.
  sl_price   the stop and target as PRICES - the same levels TradingView
  tp_price   holds. These names are always read as prices, whatever the
             robot's Price mode is, and they are one price unit, which
             TheConnector requires (mixing units sends the order with no
             stop and no target).
  tag        the robot stamps it into the position's comment and then
             closes only positions carrying it - your manual trades and
             other strategies are never touched. Since v7.1 every trade
             has its own: the tag text plus the trade's code (v7.2:
             from the signal candle's time, smcgold-k3f9), so a close
             only ever closes its own trade. Letters, digits,
             - and _ only, 12 characters at most - keep the text to 7.
  closelong / closeshort   close only the trade carrying that tag, on
             this symbol. The strategy never sends closeall, which would
             close EVERYTHING on every symbol.

THE STEPS

  On TheConnector:
  1. Robot version 0.0.21 or later. Older robots ignore a size and stop
     written in the alert and play it with the account's own settings.
  2. Copy your access key from the dashboard. Keep it private: whoever
     has it can trade your account through TheConnector.

  In the strategy's settings:
  3. Group 21: "Round the size to the broker's lot step" = 0.01.
  4. Group 22: "Attach a webhook message to every order" ON, "Message
     format" TheConnector, "Quantity to send" Lots, "Minimum lot" 0.01.
     Leave "License ID / account tag" BLANK (the key goes in the URL).
  5. Group 30: "Symbol to send" = the exact gold symbol in your FxPro
     MT5 Market Watch (blank sends XAUUSD). Tag: leave smcgold. Stop /
     target fields: sl_price / tp_price.

  In TradingView:
  6. Alerts -> Create alert -> Condition: SMC STRAT, "Order fills".
  7. Message: ONLY  {{strategy.order.alert_message}}
     NOT the sample message from TheConnector's dashboard - that is one
     fixed "buy", and every fill would open another buy.
  8. Notifications: Webhook URL =
        https://webhook.theconnector.fr/YOUR_ACCESS_KEY
  9. Save. One alert covers every buy, sell and close.

  Then:
  10. Demo account first. On the first trade, check in MT5: the lot,
      the stop price and the target price must be exactly what the
      strategy shows on the chart.

If you would rather keep the key in the message (as the dashboard's own
sample does): type it in "License ID / account tag" and use the plain
URL https://webhook.theconnector.fr/ - every message then ends with
"accessKey":"...".

Two things TheConnector does that you should know:
  - A late command expires and is not replayed. If TradingView's alert
    reaches it very late, that trade is simply not taken - the backtest
    will show a trade the account does not have.
  - A close whose tag never appeared on an entry closes nothing. Keep the
    same tag for as long as a trade can be open.

Not used, on purpose: TheConnector's break-even and trailing parameters
(betrigger, trailtrig, atr...) and newsltp. The backtest assumes the stop
and target never move; moving them at the broker would make live results
differ from the backtest.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW ORDER CANCELLATION WORKS IN AUTOMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Short answer: there is nothing to cancel at the broker, so nothing needs to
be sent.

The strategy works with limit orders - the pullback level for rule 1, the
broken pivot for rule 2 - and it cancels them constantly. On a new signal,
on the session ending, on the expiry timer, on the weekend cutoff. In the
backtest you will see far more setups armed than orders filled.

None of that ever reaches your broker, because A MESSAGE IS ONLY SENT WHEN
AN ORDER FILLS, never when it is placed. So while a setup is waiting, the
limit order exists ONLY inside TradingView. Your broker knows nothing about
it. When the strategy cancels it, the order simply disappears from
TradingView and the broker never had anything to cancel.

    armed        -> nothing sent, broker sees nothing
    cancelled    -> nothing sent, broker still sees nothing
    FILLED       -> one buy or sell message, broker opens the trade

So all of these work automatically and need nothing from your bridge:
cancel on a new signal, the N-bar expiry, the session window closing, the
weekend cutoff, the stop-distance skip, the minimum-lot skip.

The consequence you do need to understand: your broker entry is a MARKET
order, sent after TradingView has already confirmed the fill. Price touched
your limit and then the message went out. So the real entry will be slightly
worse than the backtest, every time. That is the price of this design, and
it is the normal one.

ONE THING NOT TO DO. Do not configure your bridge to place PENDING orders -
buylimit, selllimit, buystop, sellstop. If it does, a real resting order
exists at the broker, and the strategy has no way to tell it to cancel,
because cancellations do not fire alerts. You would end up with stale
pending orders at the broker that the strategy believes it killed hours
ago. Market orders on fill only.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPEED  -  what v4.0 made faster, and what it deliberately did not touch
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
FASTER:

  - An order is only re-sent when something about it actually changed -
    entry, stop, target or size. v3.x re-submitted the identical order,
    bracket and webhook text on every bar a setup was waiting. In the
    simulation that cut order submissions by 54%.
  - The bracket is attached once at entry instead of being rewritten on
    every bar of a running trade.

Verified: across 240 random settings combinations, v4.0 took EXACTLY the
same trades as the unoptimised version - same bars, same prices, same
sizes, same exits. The speed-up changes nothing you trade.

NOT TOUCHED, ON PURPOSE: the structure engine itself. The strategy only
trades main-tier signals, but the file still runs the internal and inner
tiers too, and that is most of the work on every bar. It would be the
biggest speed win - and I did not take it. The three tiers share pivot
lists and inducement logic, and the higher-timeframe filter reads internal
levels in its own calculation. Cutting the unused tiers out could silently
change which main-tier signals fire, and there is no way to prove it does
not without TradingView itself. A faster load is not worth trades that
quietly differ from the indicator you have been checking them against.

WHAT THIS STRATEGY WILL NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - It will not hold a long and a short at once. Platform limit.
  - It will not trade internal or inner structure. Main tier only.
  - It will not move your stop to breakeven or trail it. The bracket is
    set at entry and stays there.
  - It will not add to a position except with a trade for a same-side BOS
    (v7.1). It never adds on a CHOCH, and never to average down a loser.
  - It will not guess a stop. No opposite level, no trade.
  - It will not protect you from a martingale ladder you switched on
    yourself. The hard cap is the only thing standing there.

EVERY BUG FOUND SO FAR, v1.0 TO v7.2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  v1.0  Stale setup fired after a trade closed. A same-direction signal
        while already in a trade armed a setup that fired later. Setups are
        now only accepted when flat, or on an opposite signal being reversed.

  v1.0  Stop ended up on the wrong side of a resting entry. The sanity
        check declined to re-place the order but the old one stayed live.
        The setup is now cancelled outright.

  v2.0  Only 2 of 133 orders filled. TradingView was silently rejecting
        every order larger than the account. Fixed with zero declared
        margin plus the leverage cap.

  v2.0  The "size capped" counter incremented every bar instead of once
        per episode. Latched.

  v2.0  Permanent deadlock: the risk-cap halt could only be cleared by a
        winning trade, but the halt blocked every entry, so no trade could
        open to produce that win. Replaced with the three-way cap action
        and a daily reset.

  v2.1  The force-close needed a bar in the exact flat hour. A weekend or a
        broker break meant it never ran. Replaced with the dead-zone test.

  v2.2  A variable was used before it was declared. Pine is strict about
        declaration order - the file would not have compiled. Caught before
        release.

  v2.3  ZERO TRADES ON 1D, 1W AND 1M. The session window judged a bar by
        its own timestamp, which on a daily chart is a single fixed moment.
        For gold that moment sits outside a normal session window, so every
        bar on the chart was rejected. Fixed by the Auto time-filter mode.

  v2.3  The 1h to 12h charts were quietly losing signals for the same
        reason - a bar was judged by where it OPENED, not by the hours it
        actually covers. 4h went from 66.7% of bars allowed to 83.3%.

  v2.3  The recovery ladder reset on any profit, however small. A +5.53
        exit wiped several hundred dollars of carried losses. Now it
        tracks what is still unrecovered.

  v2.3  Commission was not subtracted before a trade was counted toward
        the ladder and the daily loss limit.

  v3.0  Commission was not in the position size at all. Risking "50" on a
        3-point gold stop actually cost 59.33 - 18.7% more than you asked
        for, and the tighter the stop the worse it got. Sizing now includes
        the round-turn charge.

  v3.0  Even with sizing fixed, a winner paid less than a loser cost,
        because the fee comes off the win as well as adding to the loss. On
        a 3-point stop a 1:1 "winner" netted 34.27 against a 50.00 loser.
        The target now covers the charge.

  v3.1  THE WEBHOOK SENT 100x THE POSITION SIZE. PineConnector's volume=
        is in LOTS, the strategy was sending UNITS. An 8.28-ounce gold
        trade went out as "volume=8.28", which is 8.28 lots - 828 ounces.
        Live, on a real account, that is an account-ending order. Fixed
        with the "Quantity to send" setting, which divides by the contract
        size and defaults to Lots.

  v3.1  JSON mode sent invalid JSON on every exit. The weekend, time-flat
        and opposite-signal closes have no stop or target, so the message
        came out as "sl":NaN,"tp":NaN. Any normal parser rejects that, so
        the close would never have reached the broker - leaving a live
        position the strategy believed it had closed. Now sends null.

  v3.1  PineConnector mode sent the command "close", which does not exist
        in PineConnector. Same outcome: exits silently ignored. Now sends
        closelong or closeshort.

  v3.1  The alert setup in these notes said "Any alert() function call".
        That is the indicator wording. For a strategy it is "Order fills".
        Following the old instruction would have produced an alert that
        never fired on a trade.

  v3.2  The backtest traded sizes no broker would accept. TradingView
        filled 0.0828 lots; a real account takes 0.08. Every figure in the
        report was therefore slightly wrong. Sizes are now rounded to the
        broker's lot step before the order is placed.

  v3.2  Found in simulation before release, not in the wild: rounding UP
        at the smallest sizes could raise the real risk by 50%. 0.0066
        lots rounds to 0.01, and on a 300-point gold stop that is 300 of
        risk instead of 200. Capped by the overshoot limit.

  v4.0  The full audit. Eleven bugs - each one explained at the top of
        these notes under "WHAT THE v4.0 AUDIT FOUND": same-bar trades the
        strategy never saw (and could take twice), Rule B stopping forever
        on a small debt, commission counted twice, the higher timeframe
        repainting live, every time rule one bar late, the broker's target
        drifting from TradingView's, halts blocking the protective exit, a
        pending setup surviving a trend flip, a stale setup waking up the
        next day, the indicator's alerts reaching the webhook, and tiny
        orders printed as qty 0.

  v4.1  The counter table. "Orders filled" counted TradingView's margin-
        call slices as new trades (54 armed, 100 filled on your chart),

  - Every webhook message the strategy can produce - three formats x six
    order types - was generated and checked. Every JSON message parses.
    Every PineConnector command is a real command. No NaN anywhere. Lot
    conversion checked on gold and on a forex pair.

Simulation is not a compiler and it is not a broker. It proves the logic is
internally consistent. It cannot prove the strategy makes money. Test on
paper first.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT COULD STILL BE BETTER  -  options for later, not done yet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. REAL PENDING ORDERS AT THE BROKER. The one gap no setting closes: the
     broker enters at market after TradingView's limit fills. The fix is to
     send the pending limit itself to FxPro when the setup arms, and a
     cancel when TradingView cancels it. It needs a bridge that supports
     pending orders AND cancellations, and it is only safe in "Send once"
     mode. I have not built it until we know exactly what your bridge
     (WebhookTrade or another) accepts - a lost cancel message would leave
     a live order at the broker that the strategy thinks is gone.

  2. CHART YOUR BROKER'S OWN FEED. If FxPro's TradingView connection works
     with your account, charting FxPro gold removes the OANDA-versus-FxPro
     price difference entirely. No code needed.

  3. BAR MAGNIFIER. TradingView Premium can resolve fills inside a bar
     from lower-timeframe data instead of guessing high-first or low-first.
     The simulation showed the guess rarely changes a 1-minute trade, so
     this is a small gain - but it is free if your plan has it.

  4. A LESS FRICTION-SENSITIVE TIMEFRAME. Every cost here - spread,
     commission, delay - is a fixed amount per trade. On 1-minute gold with
     3 to 5 point stops that is a large slice of your risk. On 5-minute,
     with wider stops, the same costs are a much smaller slice. Worth a
     backtest with the spread model on before deciding.
